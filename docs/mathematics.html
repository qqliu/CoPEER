From a failure to the mathematics

Asking why a counterexample works

A checked counterexample does more than stop a broken proof. Each case below began with one witness and ended with a statement about every case: the exact failure set of a printed algorithm, a classification and count of frozen colourings, the unique optimum of a family of integer colourings, and the dimensions in which a layered construction beats a conjectured bound. The initial graph and integer-colouring witnesses came from model runs without access to BugBank; with the help of AI tools, we developed the family-level results, each proved in the paper and, where stated, certified in Lean.

The integer square-root recurrence

The printed algorithm is intended to compute $\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor$ for a nonnegative integer $x$; it starts with answer $0$ at $0$ and, given answer $b$ at $x-1$, returns $b$ if $x\le(b+1)^2$ and $b+1$ otherwise. Proposition. For every positive input $x$ the printed algorithm returns $\lfloor\sqrt{x-1}\rfloor$, lagging the true integer square root by one input; it is wrong exactly at the positive perfect squares. Replacing $\le$ with $<$, the correction proposed in the source, gives $\lfloor\sqrt{x}\rfloor$ for every nonnegative integer. Induction proves both statements.

outside the human-approved core The original dossier certifies the failure at $x=1$; it is not in the core because a human reviewer questioned its reading of the source. The classification is proved in the appendix and in mathematics/extensions/sqrt_boundary/.

Frozen six-colourings of doubled odd cycles

Replace each vertex of the cycle $C_n$ by a pair of adjacent vertices and join each vertex of a pair to both vertices of the neighbouring pairs: this is $C_n[K_2]$. A proper colouring is frozen when every vertex already sees all the other colours among its neighbours, so no single vertex can change colour. Proposition. Let $n\ge5$ be odd. The doubled $n$-cycle has a frozen six-colouring exactly when $n$ is a multiple of three. For each such $n$, there are exactly $90\cdot2^n$ such colourings when the vertices and six colours are labelled: $\binom62\binom42=90$ ways to assign disjoint colour pairs to the first three bags, and two ways to order the pair inside each of the $n$ labelled bags.

The 18-vertex $C_9[K_2]$ needs five colours yet has a frozen six-colouring, which refutes a conjecture on the recolourability of clique blowups of prime graphs whose induced subgraphs are all recolourable. The existence construction was known (Shaebani, 2009); necessity and the count are new. Larger bags: for odd $n\ge9$ and $q=\lceil n/3\rceil$, $C_n[K_q]$ has frozen $n$-colourings. Lean: mathematics/theorems/CycleBlowupAllN.lean, AllOdd.lean, Q3Law.lean.

The two-threshold Schur colourings

Colour every even integer in $\{1,\dots,n\}$ red; colour an odd integer $k$ blue if $k/n\le a$ or $k/n>b$ and green if $a<k/n\le b$. Theorem. With $a=4/11$ and $b=10/11$, the colouring at $n=22m$ has $99m^2+3m$ rainbow ordered Schur triples $(x,y,x+y)$ out of $n(n-1)/2$, a fraction converging to $9/22\approx40.9\%>2/5$. Proposition. Over $0\le a\le b\le1$ the limiting fraction $D(a,b)$ is piecewise quadratic with unique maximum $9/22$ at $(4/11,10/11)$; on the region $a\le\tfrac12\le b$, $a+b\ge1$, writing $u=a-\tfrac4{11}$ and $v=b-\tfrac{10}{11}$, $\tfrac9{22}-D(a,b)=\tfrac52u^2-2uv+\tfrac32v^2\ge\tfrac{4-\sqrt5}{2}(u^2+v^2)$. The simpler rules with $a=0$ or $b=1$ reach at most $2/5$.

The optimum is within the two-threshold family; a higher limiting fraction requires a rule outside it. Independent work by Hegde, Kumar and Pratibha (2026) obtained the same construction and lower bound together with a global upper bound of $8/15$; the saved model run is dated 4 September 2026, the preprint 16 September 2026, and the paper leaves mathematical priority unassigned. Lean: mathematics/theorems/SchurTemplateComposed.lean, mathematics/supporting/sources/RainbowSchur.lean.

Projective cubes in cyclic groups

In $\mathbb{Z}_N$ the nonempty subset sums of $d$ generators form a projective $d$-cube (repeated and zero generators allowed); a cube-free set contains no such cube. The source conjectures that when $d$ divides $N$ the density of a cube-free set is at most $(d-1)/d$. Certified families. For every integer $k\ge1$: $d=5$, $N=80k$, excluded residues $\{0,4,12\}$ modulo $16$, density $13/16>4/5$; $d=7$, $N=168k$, residues $\{0,6,18\}$ modulo $24$, density $7/8>6/7$; $d=8$, $N=72k$, residues $\{0,6,12,30\}$ modulo $36$, density $8/9>7/8$. Boundary of the layered construction. Write $C_d\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{2^n}$ for the recursively defined layered construction of Long and Wagner; it fits in the group when all its layer indices are at most $n$, which $d\le n$ guarantees. Let $g(1)=1$ and $g(P+m-1)=(1+g(m))/(2P)$ for $P=2^{\ell}$, $\ell\ge1$, $1\le m\le P$. The Lean proofs establish, for every $d\ge2$ and every $n$ for which the layers fit, that $|C_d|/2^n=1-g(d)$ and that this density exceeds $(d-1)/d$ exactly when $d\notin\{2^{\ell}-2,\,2^{\ell}-1,\,2^{\ell}:\ell\ge1\}$, with equality exactly at the powers of two. Cube-freeness of the general $C_d$ is Long and Wagner's result and enters the conditional Lean theorems as an explicit premise; the certificates for $d=5,7,8$ verify cube-freeness directly.

The $d=5$ set is the pullback of the published layered set and was rediscovered by the search; we found the $d=7$ and $d=8$ sets by computer search, with AI tools, on 19 September 2026, and then formalized them; their construction priority is not established. Lean: mathematics/theorems/Cube5Mod16.lean, Cube7Mod24.lean, Cube8Mod36.lean; boundary results in mathematics/extensions/layered_boundary/.

Further certified families

Rook placements on a permutation grid

For the permutation $w=(2,7,4,8,1,6,3,5)$, the Lean kernel computes $155$ complete non-attacking rook placements on its permutation crossword grid; since $155\equiv3\pmod4$, this count violates the necessity direction of the source's Conjecture 3.10. A placement puts exactly one rook in every maximal horizontal and vertical white segment, singletons included; black cells block attacks, so a placement has $14$ rooks rather than one per row.

The other certified families

Supertoken graphs (resolving sets of size at most $n-2$ for every $n\ge8$, against $\dim F_2(K_n)=n-1$); binomial polynomials (no coefficientwise $q$-log-convexity for every integer $m\ge4$); fake-Gaussian positivity (an exact integer polynomial with a negative coefficient); $q$-rious unimodality (a non-unimodal coefficient sequence, with the Landau hypothesis certified); Toda numerators (a numerator that is not unimodal under any common-exponent regrading). Each is listed with its refuted statement, toolchain and citation in the front page's family cards (Mathematics section, fourth tab); scope, certificate file, provenance and the human judgments on each dossier are in the paper.
