# A negative q squared coefficient in Sun’s second q-log-convexity assertion

> Deposition record dated 11 September 2026. Read [later mathematical and attribution updates](../../README.md#later-updates) alongside this original record.

## Object and why it works

The second assertion requires coefficientwise nonnegativity of
$V_{r-1,m}V_{r+1,m}-V_{r,m}^2$ for every integer $m\ge2$ and
central index $r\ge1$, where
$V_{r,m}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom{r+j}{j}^m q^j$.
Take $m=11,r=3$. The binomial bases for the three polynomials are
$(1,3,6)$, $(1,4,10,20)$, and $(1,5,15,35,70)$.
Consequently

$$q^2 (see the saved provenance in the discovery index)=2(15^{11})+6^{11}=17299874515806,$$
$$[q^2]V_{3,11}^2=2(10^{11})+16^{11}=17792186044416.$$

Their difference is $-492311528610<0$. The checker independently computes
binomial coefficients by factorials and Pascal's recurrence, and compares
full polynomial multiplication with direct coefficient extraction.
The witness refutes the second universal assertion and therefore the
conjunction in the recorded target. It does not separately refute or
settle the remaining three assertions.

## What it kills

For every integer m>=2, V_(r,m)(q)=sum_(j=0)^r binom(r+j,j)^m q^j is q-log-convex for r>=0: every coefficient of V_(r-1,m)V_(r+1,m)-V_(r,m)^2 is nonnegative for each central index r>=1 (second assertion of Conjecture 1.1).

This is the statement in Conjecture 1.1, second assertion of the exact source version cited below,
with the source definitions retained in the vendored input.

## Debugging uses

Track which conjunct fails and preserve central-index ranges; numerical positivity of a polynomial at real q is weaker than coefficientwise positivity.
