## Object and why it works

Define $F=\prod_{i=1}^{n}((1-q^{m+i})/(1-q^i))^{a_i}$.
Take $n=21,m=22$, with the nonzero entries
$a_6=1$, $a_{14}=a_{18}=a_{20}=2$, and $a_{21}=1$.
The denominator exponents, with multiplicity, are
$(6,14,14,18,18,20,20,21)$, and the numerator exponents are
$(28,36,36,40,40,42,42,43)$.

Exact integer polynomial division gives zero remainder and a degree 176
quotient. Independently, its nonzero cyclotomic valuations are
$e_4=3,e_6=1,e_8=2,e_{12}=2,e_{14}=1,e_{21}=1,e_{28}=1,
e_{36}=2,e_{40}=2,e_{42}=2,e_{43}=1$; all are nonnegative, proving
polynomiality. Above 43 every valuation is zero.

The checker reconstructs all coefficients by long division and independently
by a forward recurrence. The coefficients at degrees 43 and 133 are both
$-1$. For the degree 43 coefficient, the denominator reciprocal has zero
coefficients at $43,15,7,3,1$, leaving just the numerator term $-q^{43}$.
Every parameter satisfies the source's stated domain, including $m\ge n$.
Polynomiality at other values of $m$ and symmetry of the vector are not
hypotheses of this target.

## What it kills

For every n>=1, every m>=n and every (a1,...,an) in Z_nonnegative^n, if F(m,a;q) is a polynomial then all its coefficients are nonnegative. No symmetry condition on a and no polynomiality requirement at other values of m is imposed.

This is the statement in Conjecture 4 of the exact source version cited below,
with the source definitions retained in the vendored input.

## Debugging uses

Distinguish polynomiality from coefficient positivity; retaining every factor multiplicity is essential.
