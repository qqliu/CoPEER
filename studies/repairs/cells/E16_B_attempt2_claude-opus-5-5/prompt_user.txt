=== PROBLEM / QUESTION (verbatim, stored text) ===
P0001 | Title: Angle is not Invariant under Affine Transformation
P0002 | 
P0003 | The [[Definition:Angle|angle]] between two [[Definition:Straight Line|(straight) lines]] does not necessarily stay the same under an [[Definition:Affine Transformation|affine transformation]].

=== REPLY / POST TEXT (verbatim, stored text) ===
0001 | In order to prove this, we can supply a counter example. Consider the transformation:
0002 | \[ \LL : \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2 \\ \LL : \paren {x, y} \mapsto \paren{x, x + y} \]
0003 | 
0004 | We can check that $\LL$ is an affine transformation. Indeed, if we denote $A = \paren{x_A, y_A}$ and $B = \paren{x_B, y_B}$ two points of the [[Definition:Euclidean Plane|Euclidean plane]], then we have:
0005 | 
0006 | \[ \LL (A) - \LL (B) = \paren{x_A - x_B, x_A - x_B + y_A - y_B} = \paren{x_\vec{AB}, x_\vec{AB} + y_\vec{AB}}\]
0007 | This final expression being a linear transformation of $\vec{AB}$, it follows that $\LL$ is indeed an affine transformation.
0008 | 
0009 | Furthermore, the [[Definition:Axis/X-Axis|$x$-axis]] represented by the equation $Ox: y=0$ is transformed into $\LL (Ox) : x + y = 0$, and the [[Definition:Axis/Y-Axis|$y$-axis]] represented by the equation $Oy: x=0$ is transformed into $\LL (Oy) : x = 0$.
0010 | 
0011 | Finally putting the pieces together, we see that $\angle \paren{Ox,Oy} = \frac{\pi}{2} \ne \frac{\pi}{4} = \angle \paren{\LL (Ox), \LL (Oy)}$.
0012 | That is to say, the angle between two lines does not necessarily stay the same under an affine transformation.
