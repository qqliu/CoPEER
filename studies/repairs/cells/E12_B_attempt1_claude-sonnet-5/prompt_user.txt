0003 | my work so far: 
0004 | suppose n is a particular but arbitrarily chosen integer. by the quotient remainder theorem, n can be expressed as n = d*q + r where d, q, r are integers and r is greater than or equal to 0 and less than d. choose d = 6. then n = d*q + r where r is greater than or equal to 0 and less than 6 so n = (6q) v (6q + 1) v (6q + 2) v (6q + 3) v (6q + 4) v (6q + 5). 6q = 2(3q) so is even. 6q + 2 = 2(3q +2) so is even. 6q + 3 = 3(2q + 1) so is divisible by 3. 6q + 4 = 2(3q + 2) so is even. Thus none of those four cases can describe all prime numbers since they have divisors other than 1. Since n must describe all integers by QRT, (6q + 1) and (6q + 5) must describe the prime numbers. but since they  are not divisible by 2 or 3, they cannot describe 2 or 3.
