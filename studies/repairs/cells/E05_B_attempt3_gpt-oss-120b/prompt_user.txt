P0001 | Master theorem not applicable?
P0002 | 
P0003 | Given the following recursive equation
P0004 | 
P0005 | $$ T(n) = 2T\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)+n\log n$$ we want to apply the Master theorem and note that
P0006 |

P0008 | 
P0009 | Now we check the first two cases for $\varepsilon > 0$, that is whether

P0011 |  * $n\log n \in O(n^{1-\varepsilon})$ or
P0012 |  * $n\log n \in \Theta(n)$.
P0013 | 
P0014 | The two cases are not satisfied. So we have to check the third case, that is whether
P0015 | 
P0016 |  * $n\log n \in \Omega(n^{1+\varepsilon})$ .
P0017 | 
P0018 | I think the third condition is not satisfied either. But why? And what would be a good explanation for why the Master theorem cannot be applied in this case?

=== REPLY / POST TEXT (verbatim, stored text) ===
0001 | The three cases of the Master Theorem that you refer to are proved in the _Introduction to Algorithms_ by Thomas H. Cormen,Charles E. Leiserson,Ronald L. Rivest and Clifford Stein (2nd Edition, 2001).
0002 | 
0003 | It is correctly observed that the recurrence in question falls between Case 2 and Case 3. That is $f(n) = n \log n$ grows faster than $n$ but slower than $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for any $\varepsilon > 0$.
0004 | 
0005 | However the theorem can be generalized to cover this recurrence. Consider
0006 | 
0007 | **Case 2A:**
0008 | Consider $f(n) = \Theta (n^{\log_b a }\log_b^k n)$ for some $k\geq 0$.
0009 | 
0010 | This case reduces to Case 2 when $k = 0$. It is intuitively clear that along each branch of the recurrence tree $f(x)$ is being added $\Theta (\log_b n)$ times. A sketch of a more formal proof can be found below. The final result is that
0011 | 
0012 | $$T(n) = \Theta (n^{\log_b a }\log_b^{k+1} n).$$
0013 | 
0014 | In the _Introduction to algorithms_ this statement is left as an exercise.
0015 | 
0016 | Applying this statement to the recurrence in question we finally get
0017 | 
0018 | $$
0019 |     T(n) = \Theta(n \cdot \log^2 n).
0020 | $$
0021 | 
0022 | More details on the Master Theorem can be found in the excellent (imho) [Wikipedia page][1].
0023 | 
0024 | As @sdcvvc points in the comments to prove that Case 3 does not apply here one can invoke L'Hospital's rule that says that
0025 | 
0026 | $$
0027 |    \lim_{x\to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x\to c} \frac{f^\prime(x)}{g^\prime(x)}
0028 | $$
0029 | 
0030 | for any functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ differentiable in the vicinity of $c$. Applying this to $f(n) = n \log n$ and $g(n) = n^{1+\varepsilon}$ one can show that $\log n \not\in \Theta (n^{1+\varepsilon}).$
0031 | 
0032 | ---
0033 | 
0034 | Sketch of the Proof of the Master Theorem for Case 2A.
0035 | 
0036 | This is a reproduction of parts of the proof from _Introduction to Algorithms with the necessary modifications_.
0037 | 
0038 | First we prove the following Lemma.
0039 | 
0040 | **Lemma A:**
0041 | 
0042 | Consider a function
0043 | 
0044 | $$
0045 |  g(n) = \displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{\log_b {n-1}} a^j h(n/b^j)
0046 | $$
0047 | 
0048 | where $h(n) = n^{\log_b a} \log_b^k n.$ Then
0049 | $g(n) = n^{\log_b a} \log_b^{k +1} n.$
0050 | 
0051 | **Proof:**
0052 | Substituting the $h(n)$ into the expression for $g(n)$ one can get
0053 | $$g(n) = \displaystyle n^{\log_b a} \log_b^k n \; \sum_{j=0}^{\log_b{n-1}}  \left(\frac{a}{b^{\log_b a}}\right)^j = n^{\log_b a} \log_b^{k +1} n.$$
0054 | 
0055 | **QED**
0056 | 
0057 | If $n$ is an exact power of $b$ given a recurrence
0058 | 
0059 | $$
0060 |  T(n) = a T(n/b) + f(n),\quad  T(1) = \Theta(1)
0061 | $$
0062 | 
0063 | one can rewrite it as
0064 | 
0065 | $$
0066 |  T(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_b a}) + \displaystyle \sum_{j=0}^{\log_b n - 1} a^j f(n/b^j).
0067 | $$
0068 | 
0069 | Substituting $f(n)$ with $\Theta(n^{\log_b a} \log_b^k n)$, moving $\Theta$ outside and applying Lemma A we get
0070 | 
0071 | $$T(n) = \Theta (n^{\log_b a }\log_b^{k+1} n).$$
0072 | 
0073 | Generalizing this to an arbitrary integer $n$ that is not a power of $b$ is beyond the scope of this post.
0074 | 
0075 | [1]: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_theorem#Case_2
