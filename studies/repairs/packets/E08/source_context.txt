=== PROBLEM / QUESTION (verbatim, stored text) ===
P0001 | The Greatest Number of Edges on a Bipartite Graph

=== REPLY / POST TEXT (verbatim, stored text) ===
0001 | Let $G$ be a bipartite graph on $p$ vertices. Find a formula in terms of $p$ that determines the greatest number of edges that $G$ could have. Prove that this formula is correct.
0002 | 
0003 | Let $V$ be the set of vertices for $G$. Then $\lvert V \rvert = p$.
0004 | 
0005 | Let $M$ be the set of vertices of group one for $G$.
0006 | 
0007 | Let $N$ be the set of vertices of group two for $G$.
0008 | 
0009 | The maximum number of edges that $G$ can have is:
0010 | 
0011 | $$\lvert M\rvert \lvert N\rvert$$
0012 | 
0013 | Implying that every vertex in group one is adjacent to every vertex in group two.
0014 | 
0015 | Imagine $\lvert M\rvert$ and $\lvert N \rvert$ as sides of a rectangle. Then such a rectangle would have a perimeter equal to $2p$, and an area of $\lvert M\rvert \lvert N\rvert$.
0016 | 
0017 | Using the perimeter to solve for $\lvert N \rvert = p - \lvert M\rvert$. Sub this value into the formula for Area and take the derivative to maximize the area of the rectangle:
0018 | 
0019 | $$\frac{dA}{d\lvert M\rvert} = p-2\lvert M \rvert$$
0020 | $$\frac{p}{2} = \vert M \rvert$$
0021 | 
0022 | Note that $N \cup M = V$, and so $\lvert M \rvert + \lvert N \rvert = p$
0023 | 
0024 | Subbing in $\frac{p}{2}$ for $\lvert M \rvert$, it is revealed that $\lvert N \rvert = \lvert M \rvert$.
0025 | 
0026 | Therefore the formula for the greatest number of edges on a bipartite graph on $p$ edges is $\frac{p^2}{4}$. $\blacksquare$
0027 | 
0028 | Is this a valid proof? Any advice would be appreciated!
