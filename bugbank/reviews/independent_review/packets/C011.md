46 | \section{Introduction}
47 | 
48 | An \emph{association scheme} consists of a finite set $\Omega$
49 | and a collection of non-empty binary relations $R_1,\ldots,R_r$ (where $r$ is
50 | the \emph{rank} of the scheme) such that
51 | \begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
52 | \item the relations form a partition of $\Omega^2$;
53 | \item one of the relations (say $R_1$) is equality;
54 | \item all relations are symmetric;
55 | \item given $i,j,k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$, there is a non-negative integer
56 | $p_{ij}^k$ such that, for all $(a,b)\in R_k$, there are exactly $p_{ij}^k$
57 | points $c\in\Omega$ such that $(a,c)\in R_i$ and $(c,b)\in R_j$.
58 | \end{enumerate}
59 | In the literature, conditions (b) and (c) are sometimes relaxed. But it is
60 | important for our application to keep these conditions. (See~\cite{cap} for
61 | discussion of this point.)
62 | 
63 | Representing relations $R_i$ by matrices $A_i$, the conditions in the
64 | definition can be expressed as follows:
65 | \begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
66 | \item $A_1+\cdots+A_r=J$, where $J$ is the all-$1$ matrix;
67 | \item $A_1=I$, the identity matrix;
68 | \item each matrix $A_i$ is symmetric;
69 | \item for any $i,j,k$, we have $\displaystyle{A_iA_j=\sum_{k=1}^rp_{ij}^kA_k}$;
70 | so the span of $A_1,\ldots,A_r$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is an algebra.
71 | \end{enumerate}
72 | See \cite{book} for more on association schemes.

117 | \section{The pre-association scheme}
118 | 
119 | We begin by constructing a ``pre-association scheme'' on the set $G^n$ (a
120 | structure satisfying conditions (a), (c) and (d) of the definition, but with
121 | (b) replaced by ``$R_1$ is an equivalence relation on $\Omega$''). Then
122 | factoring out the equivalence relation gives the required association scheme.
123 | 
124 | Our structure has one relation $R_\pi$ for each partition $\pi$ of the set
125 | $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, defined by the rule that for two $n$-tuples
126 | $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ and $b=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)$,
127 | \[a\mathrel{R_\pi}b\hbox{ if and only if }
128 | (a_ib_i^{-1}=a_jb_j^{-1})\Leftrightarrow(i\equiv_\pi j),\]
129 | where $i\equiv_\pi j$ means that $i$ and $j$ belong to the same part of $\pi$.
130 | The matrices of these relations are symmetric and sum to the all-$1$ matrix.
131 | We prove that their span over $\mathbb{R}$ is closed under multiplication.
132 | 
133 | Let $A_\pi$ be the matrix of the relation $R_\pi$, the $|G|^n\times|G|^n$
134 | matrix whose $(a,b)$ entry is $1$ if $a\mathrel{R_\pi}b$, $0$ otherwise.
135 | Let $B_\pi$ be the matrix of the relation $S_\pi$ defined by
136 | \[a\mathrel{S_\pi}b\hbox{ if and only if }
137 | (i\equiv_\pi j)\Rightarrow(a_ib_i^{-1}=a_jb_j^{-1}).\]
138 | Thus we have
139 | \[B_\pi=\sum_{\pi\preceq\sigma}A_\sigma,\]
140 | where $\preceq$ is the order in the lattice of partitions of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$
141 | ordered by refinement, with finer partitions below coarser ones. Now
142 | M\"obius inversion in the lattice of set partitions (see
143 | Stanley~\cite[Section 3.7]{ec1}) shows that the $A_\pi$
144 | can be expressed as linear combinations of the $B_\sigma$. Thus the $A$ and
145 | $B$ matrices have the same span, and it suffices to show that the span of
146 | the $B$ matrices is closed under multiplication.
147 | 
148 | The $(a,b)$ entry of $B_\pi B_\sigma$ counts the number of $n$-tuples $c$
149 | such that $a\mathrel{S_\pi}c$ and $c\mathrel{S_\sigma}b$. This means
150 | that the ratios $a_ic_i^{-1}$ agree on parts of $\pi$, while the ratios
151 | $c_ib_i^{-1}$ agree on parts of $\sigma$. This is zero unless the ratios
152 | $a_ib_i^{-1}$ agree on parts of $\tau=\pi\wedge\sigma$. Assuming this does
153 | hold, we choose elements $u_i$ and $v_i$ such that $u_i$ is constant on
154 | parts of $\pi$ and $v_i$ constant on parts of $\sigma$, and such that
155 | $u_iv_i=a_ib_i^{-1}$ for all $i$.
156 | 
157 | We claim that this can be done in $|G|^{|\pi\vee\sigma|}$ ways, where $|\rho|$
158 | denotes the number of parts of $\rho$. For if we choose the value $u_i$, then
159 | the value of $v_i$ is determined. Moreover, $u_i$ is constant on parts of $\pi$,
160 | so $v_j$ is determined on these parts; and $v_j$ is constant on parts of
161 | $\sigma$, so $u_j$ is determined on these. We see that the values are
162 | determined on a part of $\pi\vee\sigma$ by the choice, and the results follow.
163 | 
164 | Now each choice as above gives a unique $c$ such that
165 | \[a_ic_i^{-1}=u_i,\qquad c_ib_i^{-1}=v_i,\]
166 | so the result is proved.

179 | \section{Taking the quotient}
180 | 
181 | The orbits of $H$ acting by left multiplication as in (a)
182 | above are precisely the right cosets of $H$ in $G^n$. Invariance of the
183 | relations above shows that the quotient of the structure by this equivalence
184 | relation has corresponding relations $\bar R_\pi$ induced on it. Now, if
185 | $\pi$ is the relation of equality, then $R_\pi$ is precisely the relation of
186 | lying in the same right coset of $H$, and so $\bar R_\pi$ is the relation
187 | of equality. So, as well as the other relations defined above, we have
188 | recovered the one missing axiom for association schemes.
189 | 
190 | Thus, we have constructed an association scheme on $H\backslash G^n$ (the
191 | set of right cosets of $H$) invariant under right translation by $G^n$ and
192 | automorphisms of $G$ acting coordinatewise.
193 | 
194 | To reach the full diagonal group we need to adjoin the symmetric group. This
195 | clearly does not preserve the relations $\bar R_\pi$, but it induces ``weak
196 | automorphisms'' of the association scheme; that is, it permutes these
197 | relations among themselves. Taking the unions of orbits of $S_n$ on the
198 | relations $\bar R_\pi$, we obtain an association scheme, whose relations are
199 | indexed by the orbits of $S_n$ on set partitions of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$; that
200 | is, simply the partitions of the integer $n$, expressions of $n$ as a sum
201 | of positive integers with the summands in non-increasing order. See
202 | \cite[Theorem 8.17]{book}.
203 | 
204 | The valency of the relation $R_\pi$ in the pre-association scheme is the
205 | number of ordered $|\pi|$-tuples of distinct elements of $G$, and so is
206 | non-zero if and only if $|G|\ge|\pi|$. In particular, all relations are
207 | non-empty if and only if $|G|\ge n$. Passing to the quotient divides the
208 | valency by $|G|$, and merging orbits under $S_n$ multiplies by the number of
209 | set-partitions corresponding to a given partition of $n$. So, the rank is
210 | equal to to the number of partitions of $n$ into at most $|G|$ parts, and is
211 | equal to $p(n)$ if and only if $|G|\ge n$. (Thanks to Cheryl Praeger for this
212 | observation.)

226 | \section{AS-free groups}
227 | 
228 | As noted above, a permutation group is \emph{AS-free} if it preserves no
229 | non-trivial association scheme. A transitive permutation group is
230 | \emph{primitive} if it preserves no non-trivial equivalence relation on its
231 | domain; a primitive group is \emph{basic} if it preserves no Cartesian
232 | structure on its domain~\cite{ps}. Moreover, a permutation group is
233 | \emph{$2$-homogeneous} if it acts transitively on the set of $2$-element
234 | subsets of its domain; and a group $G$ is \emph{almost simple} if
235 | $T\le G\le\mathrm{Aut}(T)$ for some non-abelian finite simple group $T$.
236 | 
237 | \begin{theorem}
238 | An AS-free permutation group is primitive and basic, and is either
239 | $2$-homogeneous or almost simple.
240 | \end{theorem}
241 | 
242 | \begin{proof}
243 | This is a combination of a theorem in \cite{cap} and the result of this
244 | note. Specifically, if $G$ is imprimitive, then it preserves the
245 | \emph{group-divisible} association scheme, whose relations are ``equal'',
246 | ``same part of the $G$-invariant partition'', and the rest. If $G$ is
247 | primitive but not basic, it preserves a Hamming scheme (see~\cite{book}).
248 | If $G$ is basic, then according to the O'Nan--Scott Theorem, it is affine
249 | (that is, has an elementary abelian regular normal subgroup), or diagonal,
250 | or almost simple. If $G$ is affine, then the matrices of the $G$-orbits on
251 | pairs belong to the group algebra of the regular normal subgroup, and so
252 | commute; adding each non-symmetric matrix to its transpose then gives an
253 | association scheme, whose rank is the number of $G$-orbits on $2$-sets plus
254 | one (for the diagonal), so is trivial if and only if $G$ is $2$-homogeneous.
255 | If $G$ is contained in $D_n(T)$ for some non-abelian simple group $T$, then
256 | $G$ preserves the symmetrised conjugacy class scheme of $T$. And finally, if
257 | $G$ is contained in $D_n(T)$ for $n>2$, then $G$ preserves the association
258 | scheme constructed above, which is trivial only in the case $n=2$. (We have
259 | $|T|\ge60$, so relations corresponding to partitions with at most $60$ parts
260 | are non-empty.)
261 | \end{proof}
262 | 
263 | Note that any $2$-homogeneous group is AS-free. There exist AS-free almost
264 | simple groups which are not $2$-homogeneous, but the situation is
265 | not well understood. We record here some examples taken from \cite{cap}:

277 | \subsection{Latin squares}
278 | 
279 | A Latin square of order $n$ can be regarded as an orthogonal array of
280 | strength~$2$ with $3$ factors. If $G$ is a group, we can describe this
281 | orthogonal array very simply: it is the set of triples $(x,y,z)\in G^3$
282 | with $xyz=1$. It is clear that this set is invariant under $G^3$ acting
283 | as follows:
284 | \[(g,h,k):(x,y,z)\mapsto (h^{-1}xk,k^{-1}yg,g^{-1}zh).\]
285 | Moreover, the set also admits automorphisms of $G$ acting coordinatewise,
286 | as well as permutations of the three coordinates with a small twist:
287 | $xyz=1$ implies $yzx=1$ and $z^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}=1$, so odd permutations
288 | must be combined with inversion. So this set of triples admits $D_3(G)$.
289 | 
290 | The corresponding Latin square, as an $n\times n$ array,
291 | is obtained by indexing rows and columns by $G$ and writing $z$ in the
292 | $(x,y)$ cell if $xyz=1$. The ``traditional'' Cayley table has $z$ in position
293 | $(x,y)$ if $z=xy$. For the record, we match up these two representations with
294 | ours.
295 | \begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
296 | \item The orthogonal array definition has $xy$ entry $(xy)^{-1}$, so is obtained
297 | from the traditional one by the permutation of letters which replaces each
298 | letter by its inverse.
299 | \item Represent the cosets of $H$ in $G^3$ by noting that each coset contains
300 | a unique element of the form $(x,y,1)$; we regard this as indexing a cell
301 | $(x,y)$ of a square array. Now the relation corresponding to the
302 | partition $2+1$ of $3$ makes two of the three coordinate ratios equal. So
303 | consider two coset representatives $(a_1,a_2,1)$ and $(b_1,b_2,1)$. If the
304 | first and third coordinate ratios are equal then $a_1=b_1$; so this is the
305 | relation ``same row''. Similarly, equality of the second and third rations is
306 | the relation ``same column''. In the remaining case, $a_1b_1^{-1}=a_2b_2^{-1}$,
307 | which is equivalent to $a_2^{-1}a_1=b_2^{-1}b_1$; so this is the relation
308 | ``same letter'' if we put entry $y^{-1}x$ in cell $(x,y)$. So this Latin square
309 | is obtained from the orthogonal array form by the permutation of columns which
310 | replaces each column label by its inverse.
311 | \end{itemize}
312 | 
313 | We now extend this to higher dimensions. A group $G$ defines a Latin
314 | hypercube $L$, the set of $n$-tuples $(g_1,\ldots,g_n)$ of group elements whose
315 | product is the identity; these form an orthogonal array of strength $n-1$.
316 | The set is invariant under cyclic permutations of the group elements, and
317 | reversal together with inversion:
318 | $(g_1,\ldots,g_n)\mapsto(g_n^{-1},\ldots,g_1^{-1})$.
319 | Now we can define a map from $G^n$ to $L$ by the rule
320 | \[(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\mapsto(g_1=a_1^{-1}a_2,g_2=a_2^{-1}a_3,\ldots,g_n=a_n^{-1}a_1).\]
321 | This map is invariant under left multiplication by elements of $H$, so induces
322 | a bijection $H\backslash G^n\to L$. Now the cyclic shift on $L$ is realised
323 | by the cyclic shift on $H\backslash G^n$, while the reversal-and-inversion
324 | of $L$ is induced by reversal on $H\backslash G^n$. Thus the
325 | dihedral group is a group of weak automorphisms of the association scheme
326 | on $H\backslash G^n$ with relations $R_\pi$, and fusing orbits gives an
327 | association scheme. This association scheme does not admit the full diagonal
328 | group, since only elements of the dihedral group act as coordinate permutations.
329 | (In the case $n=3$, the diagonal group is equal to the full symmetric group.)

342 | non-isomorphic association schemes.
