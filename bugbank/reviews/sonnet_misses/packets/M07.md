## What you are asked (one row of `miss_audit.csv` per sheet)

Each sheet shows one text on which the critic's FIRST candidate line (marked `C>`) is not one of the benchmark's certified lines
(marked `>>`). The critic was told that at least one assertion in the text is false as written and was asked for a line that
contains it. The certified lines are the lines on which the text asserts the ONE proposition that a machine-checked counterexample
refutes. These markings are disclosed on purpose: this is a source-aware reading, not blind detection. Your answers are counted and
reported; they never change a benchmark score, a certified line or the sample.

QUESTION. Read the full text. Judge the assertion on the `C>` line AS WRITTEN, under the assumptions the writing itself has in force
at that point. Choose exactly one class. Apply the definitions in this order of precedence:

1. `C` ANNOTATION_PROBLEM (check this first). The marking or the source is at fault: the `C>` line asserts the SAME proposition as
   the `>>` lines (an omitted occurrence of the certified claim, including an explicit restatement inside an application), or a `>>`
   line does not assert that proposition, or the text is too garbled to read. Give the sub-type in `class_c_subtype`:
   `OMITTED_SAME_PROPOSITION_GOLD`, `WRONG_GOLD_LINE`, `GARBLED_SOURCE` or `OTHER`.
2. `A` ALTERNATIVE_LOCALIZATION. The `C>` line asserts something false as written that is a DIFFERENT proposition from the certified
   one: you can state a concrete counterexample under the assumptions in force at that line. A line that only uses the certified
   claim later, draws a different true consequence of it, or has a thin justification is NOT class A.
3. `B` FIRST_CHOICE_MISS. The `C>` line is not false as written (it is true, a mere later use, a definition, a hypothesis, setup, or
   only thinly justified). This class is about the FIRST choice only. It does not say that the critic found nothing: the second or
   third candidate (marked `c2`, `c3`) may be a certified line, and the sheet states whether it is.
4. `D` UNRESOLVED (last resort). You cannot decide between the classes from this sheet. Uncertainty is not a negative verdict: say
   what is missing in `reason`.

WHAT THE CLASS DOES NOT JUDGE. The class is about the LINE, not about the critic's own words. A line can be false as written
(class A) although the counterexample the critic supplied is wrong; the benchmark does not score the critic's `claim` and
`counterexample` fields. The optional, separate soundness question is: DOES THE CRITIC'S SUPPLIED COUNTEREXAMPLE OR ARGUMENT
CORRECTLY REFUTE ITS SELECTED FIRST-CHOICE ASSERTION AS WRITTEN, UNDER THE SOURCE'S ASSUMPTIONS? Record `YES`, `NO` or
`CANNOT_DETERMINE` in `supplied_counterexample`, and explain that judgment in `supplied_counterexample_reason`. If you do not
assess this question, leave BOTH cells blank: unanswered is missing, not NO or CANNOT_DETERMINE. A CANNOT_DETERMINE response
means you considered the question but could not decide. It never changes the class. Class A is reported as an alternative
localization, not as a valid criticism. A false selected line alone does not validate the supplied objection.

The recorded judgments and generic LLM reviewer roles are supplied separately in this directory.
