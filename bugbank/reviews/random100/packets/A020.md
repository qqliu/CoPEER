\begin{lemma}[Per-path potential bound]\label{lem:B2-decay}
Under midpoint recursion and DFS, $\Phi_{\mathrm{path}} = O(\log T)$ at all times. Consequently, the number of simultaneously active interfaces is $O(\log T)$, and at most a constant number of $O(b)$ windows are ever materialized simultaneously; thus the live window memory is $O(b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At depth $d$, the active interval length is $\ell(d)\le \lceil T/2^d\rceil$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:B1-midpoint}, so
\[
\Phi_{\mathrm{path}}\ =\ \sum_{d=0}^{D} \Big\lceil \log_2\!\big(1+\ell(d)\big)\Big\rceil
\ \le\ \sum_{d=0}^{\lceil\log_2 T\rceil} \big(\log_2(T/2^d)+1\big)
\ =\ O(\log T).
\]

102 |     \Large \textbf{Abstract}
103 | \end{center}
104 | \vspace{0.3in}
105 | We prove a square-root space simulation for deterministic multitape Turing machines, showing $\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq \mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t})]$. The key step is a Height Compression Theorem that uniformly (and in logspace) reshapes the canonical left-deep succinct computation tree for a block-respecting run into a binary tree whose evaluation-stack depth along any DFS path is $O(\log T)$ for $T=\lceil t/b\rceil$, while preserving $O(b)$ work at leaves, $O(1)$ at internal nodes, and edges that are logspace-checkable; semantic correctness across merges is witnessed by an exact $O(b)$ window replay at the unique interface. The proof uses midpoint (balanced) recursion, a per-path potential that bounds simultaneously active interfaces by $O(\log T)$, and an indegree-capping replacement of multiway merges by balanced binary combiners. Algorithmically, an Algebraic Replay Engine with constant-degree maps over a constant-size field, together with pointerless DFS and index-free streaming, ensures constant-size per-level tokens and eliminates wide counters, yielding the additive tradeoff $S(b)=O\!\big(b+\log(t/b)\big)$ \emph{for block sizes $b \ge b_0$ with $b_0=\Theta(\log t)$}, which at the canonical choice $b=\Theta(\sqrt{t})$ gives $O(\sqrt{t})$ space; the $b_0$ threshold rules out degenerate blocks where addressing scratch would dominate the window footprint. The construction is uniform, relativizes, and is robust to standard model choices. Consequences include branching-program upper bounds $2^{O(\sqrt{s})}$ for size-$s$ bounded-fan-in circuits, tightened quadratic-time lower bounds for $\mathrm{SPACE}[n]$-complete problems via the standard hierarchy argument, and $O(\sqrt{t})$-space certifying interpreters; under explicit locality assumptions, the framework extends to geometric $d$-dimensional models. Conceptually, the work isolates path bookkeeping as the chief obstruction to $O(\sqrt{t})$ and removes it via structural height compression with per-path analysis rather than barrier-prone techniques.

113 | \subsection{Problem statement and context}
114 | A prominent question in complexity theory asks: \emph{how little workspace suffices to deterministically simulate a time-$t$ computation?} For classical multitape Turing machines, the best general upper bounds have long taken the form of a tradeoff in which one chooses a block size $b$ and pays (i) $O(b)$ space to locally ``replay'' a block and (ii) an additional term that accounts for the \emph{evaluation stack depth} of an implicit computation tree and the \emph{bookkeeping} needed to navigate it.
115 | 
116 | Recent progress has established the inclusion
117 | 
118 | \begin{equation}
119 | \mathrm{TIME}[t]\ \subseteq\ \mathrm{SPACE}\!\left[O\!\left(\sqrt{t\log t}\right)\right],
120 | \end{equation}
121 | 
122 | by reducing a time-$t$ run to a \textsc{Tree Evaluation} instance of height $\Theta(t/b)$ with $O(b)$-sized per-node functions and then evaluating that tree in small space using algebraic techniques \cite{Williams2025}. (Here and throughout, our trees are the \emph{succinct computation trees induced by block-respecting simulations}, rather than arbitrary tree-evaluation instances.) The residual $\sqrt{\log t}$ factor arises from \emph{path bookkeeping}: during depth-first evaluation, the simulator must store a descriptor for each level of a root–leaf walk across $\Theta(t/b)$ levels, where per-level choices (child addressing, offset encoding within size-$b$ windows) require $O(\log b)$ bits, leading to total path storage of $O((t/b) \cdot \log b)$ and optimization giving $O(\sqrt{t \log t})$.
123 | 
124 | This work pursues the clean ``one-epsilon'' tightening that removes the lingering $\sqrt{\log t}$, achieving $\mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t})]$ by \emph{compressing the evaluation stack depth itself} and redesigning the evaluator so that per-level overhead is constant.

179 | \subsection{Machines, time blocking, and succinct computation trees}
180 | Let $M$ be a deterministic multitape Turing machine with $\tau$ work tapes over alphabet $\Gamma$. A \emph{configuration} consists of control state, head positions, and tape contents. Fix a block size $b\ge 1$ and partition the first $t$ steps into
181 | 
182 | \begin{equation}
183 | T\ \coloneqq\ \left\lceil \frac{t}{b}\right\rceil
184 | \end{equation}
185 | 
186 | \emph{time blocks} $B_1,\dots,B_T$, where $B_k$ simulates steps $[(k{-}1)b{+}1,\ \min\{kb,t\}]$. Each block touches at most $b$ cells per tape, so its \emph{local window} per tape is an interval of length $\le b$.
187 | 
188 | The \emph{per-block summary} $\sigma_k$ records exactly: (i) entering/leaving control state, (ii) entering/leaving head positions for each tape, (iii) local window boundaries and relative head positions within windows, (iv) a deterministic \emph{movement log} encoding all tape modifications as a sequence of at most $b$ edits with offsets relative to the window, and (v) exact checksums for each local window before and after the block. The movement log uses deterministic, collision-free encoding requiring $O(b)$ cells total. See Appendix~A for complete formal definitions.
189 | 
190 | For an interval $I=[i,j]\subseteq[1,T]$, the \emph{interval summary} $\Sigma(I)$ is obtained by composing the block summaries $\sigma_i,\dots,\sigma_j$ and verifying exact consistency at each interface between adjacent blocks; composition requires only bounded-window replays and deterministic state checks. The \emph{succinct computation tree} $\mathcal{T}$ used for the canonical tradeoff is the \emph{left-deep (unbalanced) binary interval tree} on $[1,T]$: its leaves are the unit intervals $[k,k]$ (labeled by $\sigma_k$), and each internal node labeled by $I=[i,j]$ stores (or recomputes) $\Sigma(I)$ from its two children via the associative merge operator $\oplus$. This tree has height $\Theta(T)$.\footnote{For comparison, the balanced interval tree on $[1,T]$ has height $\Theta(\log T)$. We fix the left-deep tree here to expose the path-bookkeeping term that underlies the classical $O\!\big(b+(t/b)\log b\big)$ space bound; our height-compression transform later reshapes the tree and schedule.}

192 | \paragraph{Why a balanced tree wasn’t previously enough.}
193 | A balanced interval tree on $[1,T]$ indeed has recursion depth $\Theta(\log T)$, but prior small‑space simulations incurred a \emph{per‑level descriptor} of size $\Theta(\log b)$ (for child addressing, offset encoding within $b$‑windows, and counters), so a depth‑$\Theta(\log T)$ traversal still paid $\Theta(\log T \cdot \log b)$ bits of \emph{path bookkeeping}. Our construction removes this multiplicative overhead by (i) adopting a \emph{pointerless} traversal that stores only a constant‑size token per level, and (ii) using \emph{index‑free} loops so that wide counters are replaced by recomputation. With these changes, balancing the recursion directly translates into $H'_{\mathrm{eval}}=O(\log T)$ \emph{without} reintroducing a per‑level $\log b$ factor.

225 | \subsection{Algebraic evaluation and low-degree extensions}
226 | To keep internal‑node workspace constant, we express each merge via a \emph{constant‑degree} vector‑valued polynomial over a \emph{constant‑size} finite field, but \emph{only for the finite‑state part} of an interval summary. Concretely, let $\pi(\Sigma(I))$ denote the constant‑size projection of the interval summary $\Sigma(I)$ that includes finite control state and a constant number of fixed‑size tags/flags (e.g., interface check bits). We define a map $F_I:\mathbb{F}^m\to\mathbb{F}^q$ with $m,q,\Delta=O(1)$ such that $F_I$ algebraically represents $\pi(\Sigma(I))$. All $b$‑dependent quantities (head offsets, addresses, and window contents) are \emph{handled exclusively by exact bounded‑window replay} and are never encoded as field elements. Correctness never depends on hash collision probabilities; interface equality is verified by exact replay at the unique junction.

The total space becomes \emph{additive} in (leaf window) $+$ (number of levels), provided path bookkeeping is $O(1)$ per level—precisely what stack depth compression enables.

237 | We will use classical \emph{black–white pebbling}, \emph{potential functions}, and \emph{centroid/cut decompositions} to control recursion depth and the number of simultaneously active interfaces.

239 | \begin{definition}[Active interfaces and stack discipline]
240 | An \emph{active interface} is the boundary state at the endpoint of a currently processed chunk during evaluation. The \emph{potential} $\Phi$ measures the total ``weight'' of all active interfaces. The \emph{stack discipline} maintains these interfaces according to the evaluation schedule.

400 | \section{Proof of the Height Compression Theorem}
401 | 
402 | In this section we give a complete proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:HCT}. We start from the canonical succinct computation tree on $T=\lceil t/b\rceil$ time blocks and perform three uniform, logspace-computable transformations: (i) a centroid-based (midpoint) recursion schedule with exact constants ensuring per-path \emph{geometric shrink} and hence \emph{logarithmic depth}; (ii) a rigorous potential-based pebbling discipline with full telescoping analysis; and (iii) an indegree-capping replacement of every multi-merge by a binary \emph{combiner} tree with detailed logspace-checkable edges.

404 | \subsection{Canonical recursion via midpoint recursion with exact constants}
405 | 
406 | \paragraph{Intervals and midpoints.}
407 | For an interval $I=[i,j]\subseteq [1,T]$ of length $\ell=j-i+1$, define the centroid (midpoint)
408 | \begin{equation}
409 | c(I)\ \coloneqq\ \Big\lfloor\frac{i+j}{2}\Big\rfloor.
410 | \end{equation}

413 | \begin{lemma}[Exact midpoint shrink and depth]\label{lem:mid-shrink}
414 | For every root-to-leaf path under midpoint recursion, if $\ell(d)$ denotes the active-interval length at depth $d$, then
415 | \[
416 | \ell(d{+}1)\ \le\ \Big\lceil \frac{\ell(d)}{2}\Big\rceil,
417 | \]
418 | and the recursion depth is at most $\lceil \log_2 T\rceil$. Along the path there is at most one active interval per depth.
419 | \end{lemma}
420 | 
421 | \begin{proof}
422 | Immediate from the definition of the midpoint split. Each step halves the length up to an additive $1$, so after at most $\lceil \log_2 T\rceil$ levels the length becomes $1$.

426 | Given $(i,j)$ in binary, a logspace transducer computes $c(I)=\lfloor (i{+}j)/2\rfloor$ and outputs the two child endpoints using $O(\log T)$ counters. Hence the schedule is uniform and logspace.

428 | \begin{lemma}[Per-path stack-height and window usage]\label{lem:stack-height}
429 | Under the midpoint recursion and DFS, the number of simultaneously active intervals (hence the evaluation stack depth) along any root-to-leaf traversal is $O(\log T)$. At most one $O(b)$ window is ever materialized at once, so additional window memory is $O(b)$ (not multiplied by $\log T$).
430 | \end{lemma}

436 | \subsection{Per-path potential design with rigorous telescoping analysis}
437 | 
438 | \paragraph{DFS stack discipline and active interfaces.}
439 | During a depth-first evaluation along any fixed root-to-leaf path, an \emph{active interface} is the boundary state (control, head positions, and checksums) at the left or right endpoint of a currently active interval on the DFS stack. The evaluator maintains a stack of active interfaces reflecting the current path, with at most two interfaces per active interval.
440 | 
441 | \paragraph{Potential function.}
442 | For an interval (or boundary) of length $\lambda$, define weight $w(\lambda) \coloneqq \lceil \log_2(1+\lambda)\rceil$. For any fixed DFS path with active interfaces $\mathcal{B}$ on the current stack, define
443 | \begin{equation}
444 | \Phi\ \coloneqq\ \sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}} w\!\big(\mathrm{len}(B)\big).
445 | \end{equation}
446 | 
447 | \begin{lemma}[Rigorous per-path potential decay]\label{lem:potential}
448 | Along any root-to-leaf traversal under midpoint recursion:
449 | \begin{enumerate}
450 |   \item The potential $\Phi=\sum_{d} \lceil \log_2(1+\ell(d))\rceil$ remains $O(\log T)$ throughout (initially $O(\log T)$; decreases by a constant every $O(1)$ levels; never increases on backtracking).
451 |   \item The number of simultaneously active interfaces is $O(\Phi)=O(\log T)$.
452 |   \item \textbf{Live window bound:} Although $O(\log T)$ interfaces may be active as \emph{metadata}, the DFS schedule materializes at most a constant number of $O(b)$ windows at once (in fact, one). Consequently, at most one $O(b)$ window is ever materialized at a time under the DFS schedule, so the live window workspace is $O(b)$. Interface metadata contributes only $O(\log T)$ additional cells. Thus the per-path workspace bound is $O\big(b + \log T\big)$.
453 | \end{enumerate}
454 | \end{lemma}
455 | 
456 | 
457 | \begin{proof}
458 | \textbf{Initialization.} At the root there are two interfaces of length $T$, so $\Phi(0)\le 2\lceil \log_2(1{+}T)\rceil=O(\log T)$.
459 | 
460 | \textbf{Monotonicity.} Backtracking pops interfaces and cannot increase $\Phi$.
461 | 
462 | \textbf{Decay.} When $\lambda$ is replaced by a child of length $\le \lceil \lambda/2\rceil$, we have
463 | \[
464 | w(\lceil \lambda/2\rceil)\ \le\ \left\lceil \log_2\!\big(1+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\big)\right\rceil\ \le\ w(\lambda)-1+O(1),
465 | \]
466 | so over every constant number of levels the total potential drops by at least a positive constant (small-$\lambda$ cases contribute only $O(1)$ in total). Hence $\Phi=O(\log T)$ throughout.
467 | 
468 | \textbf{Interface count and workspace.} Each active interval contributes at most two interfaces, so the count is $O(\Phi)$. Each interface is materialized via bounded-window replay using $O(b)$ cells; auxiliary fields are constant- or $O(\log t)$-bit values stored within these cells. Thus the live workspace is $O(b) + O(\log T)$: at most one $O(b)$ window is materialized at any time, and interface metadata contributes only $O(\log T)$ cells.
469 | 
470 | \end{proof}

479 | \begin{enumerate}
480 | \item \textbf{Addressing/topology in $O(\log t)$:} Given parent endpoints $(p,r)$ and midpoint $q$, the children $J=[p,q]$ and $J'=[q{+}1,r]$ are determined by $O(\log t)$-space arithmetic.

492 | \item \textbf{Endpoint computation:} All interval endpoints are recomputable from the root using pointerless traversal with $O(1)$ cells per level and $O(\log t)$ total scratch (additive, not multiplied by depth).

This increases the local depth by $O(\log d)$ and the local size by $O(d)$, while preserving uniform logspace computability. In our midpoint schedule, $d=2$ at all recursion nodes, so this step is typically vacuous.
