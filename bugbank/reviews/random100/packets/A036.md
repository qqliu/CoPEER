## 4. Exact released witness

~~~~text
Take S = {0}, T = {1} (disjoint one-element sets) and the bijection f : S -> T given by f = {0 |-> 1}, so f^{-1} : T -> S is {1 |-> 0}. Under the source wiki's convention (g o f)(x) = g(f(x)):

  f o f^{-1} : T -> T has graph {1 |-> 1}, i.e. domain {1} = T. So f o f^{-1} = I_T, NOT I_S. The point 1 lies in the domain of the composite but not in the domain of I_S (= {0}), so I_S is not even defined at 1; comparing mappings as (domain, codomain, graph) triples, f o f^{-1} != I_S.

  Lifting to direct image mappings: f^-> : P(S) -> P(T) and (f^{-1})^-> : P(T) -> P(S), so the composite f^-> o (f^{-1})^-> has domain P(T) = {{}, {1}} and graph {{} |-> {}, {1} |-> {1}}. That is I_{P(T)}. The proof's displayed line asserts it equals I_{P(S)}, which has domain P(S) = {{}, {0}} and is undefined at the subset {1}.

  Symmetrically, f^{-1} o f has graph {0 |-> 0} with domain {0} = S, so it equals I_S and (f^{-1})^-> o f^-> = I_{P(S)}, whereas the proof writes I_T / I_{P(T)}.

For this singleton witness, the correct identities are f∘f⁻¹=I_T and f⁻¹∘f=I_S. The selected Lean declarations verify the bijection and distinguish the composite graph and its direct image from the printed identity on S. The counterexample concerns the swapped subscripts in the proof, not the final inverse-of-direct-image theorem.
~~~~
