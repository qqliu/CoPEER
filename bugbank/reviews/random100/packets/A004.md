~~~~text
126 | \textbf{Abstract.}\quad {\sf
127 | A binary code is called a superimposed cover-free $(s,\ell)$-code
128 | if  the  code is identified by  the incidence matrix of a family of finite sets
129 | in which no intersection of  $\ell$ sets is covered by the union of
130 | $s$ others. A binary code is called a superimposed  list-decoding $s_L$-code
131 | if  the  code is identified by  the incidence matrix of a family of finite sets
132 | in which the union of any $s$ sets can cover not more than $L-1$ other sets
133 | of the family. For  $L=\ell=1$,  both of the definitions coincide
134 | and  the corresponding binary code is called a superimposed $s$-code.
135 | Our aim is to obtain new lower and upper bounds on the rate of given codes.
136 | The most interesting  result is a lower bound on the rate of
137 | superimposed cover-free $(s,\ell)$-code based on the ensemble of constant-weight
138 | binary codes. If parameter $\ell\ge1$ is fixed and $s\to\infty$, then
139 | the ratio of this lower bound to the best known upper bound converges to the
140 | limit~$2\,e^{-2}=0,271$. For the classical case $\ell=1$ and $s\ge2$,
141 | the given Statement means that our recurrent upper bound on the rate
142 | of superimposed $s$-codes obtained in 1982 is attained to within a constant
143 | factor $a$, $0,271\le a\le1$.}

150 | \section{Notations, Definitions and Results}
151 | \quad
152 | Let $N$, $t$, $s$, $L$ and $\ell$ be integers, where
153 |  $1\le s<t$, $1\le L\le t-s$, $\;1\le \ell\le t-s$. Let
154 | $\eq$ denote the equality by definition, $|A|$ -- the size of $A$ and
155 | $[N]\eq\{1,2,\dots,N\}$ - the set of integers from~$1$ to~$N$.
156 | A binary $(N\times t)$-matrix
157 | \beq{X}
158 | X=\|x_i(j)\|, \quad x_i(j)=0,1,\quad\x_i\eq(x_i(1),\dots,x_i(t)),\quad
159 | \x(j)\eq(x_1(j),\dots,x_N(j)),
160 | \eeq
161 |  $i\in[N]$, $j\in[t]$,  with $N$ rows $\x_1,\dots,\x_N$ and $t$ columns
162 | $\x(1),\dots,\x(t)$ (codewords) is called a {\em  code of length $N$  and size $t$}.
163 | The standard symbol $\bigvee$ denotes
164 | the {\em disjunct} (Boolean) sum of two binary numbers:
165 | $$
166 | 0\bigvee0=0,\quad  0\bigvee 1=1\bigvee 0 = 1\bigvee 1 = 1,
167 | $$
168 | as well as the component-wise disjunct sum of two binary columns.
169 | We say that a column
170 |  $\u$   covers column $\v$ ($\u \succeq \v$) if~$\u\bigvee \v=\u$.
171 | The standard symbol  $\lfloor a\rfloor$
172 | ($\lceil a\rceil$) will be used to denote the largest (least) integer $\le a$~($\ge a$).

175 | \textbf{Definition 1.}~\cite{mp88}.\quad
176 | A code $X$ is called a {\em superimposed cover-free $(s,\ell)$-code}
177 | (briefly, {\em CF $(s,\ell)$-code}) if for any two non-intersecting sets
178 |  $\S,\,\L\subset[t]$, $|\S|=\s$, $|\L|=\ell$, $\S\cap\L=\varnothing$,
179 | there exists a row $\x_i$, $i\in [N]$, for which
180 | $$
181 |  x_i(j)=0 \quad \text{for any}\quad j\in\S,\quad \text{and}\quad  x_i(k)=1\quad \text{for any}\quad k\in\L.
182 | $$
183 | Taking into account the evident symmetry over  $s$  and $\l$, we introduce
184 | $t_{cf}(N,s,\ell)=t_{cf}(N,\ell,s)$ - the maximal size of
185 | CF $(s,\ell)$-codes of length  $N$  and
186 | $N_{cf}(t,s,\ell)=N_{cf}(t,\ell,s)$ - the minimal length
187 | of  CF $(s,\ell)$-codes of size~$t$. We also define
188 | a {\em rate} of  CF $(s,\ell)$-codes:
189 |  \beq{Rsl}
190 |  R(s,\ell)=R(\ell,s)\eq \varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t_{cf}(N,s,\ell)}{N}\,=\,
191 |  \varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t}{N_{cf}(t,\s,\ell)}.
192 |  \eeq
193 | 
194 | \textbf{Definition 2.}~\cite{dr83}.\quad
195 | A code $X$ is called a {\em list-decoding superimposed code}
196 |  of {\em strength  $s$ and list size $L$} (briefly, {\em LD  $\;s_L$-code}), if the disjunct sum
197 | of any $s$-subset of codewords  $X$ can cover not more than  $L-1$ codewords that are not components
198 | of the given $s$-subset.
199 | Introduce  $t_{ld}(N,s,L)$ -- the maximal size of LD $\;s_L$-codes of length $N$ and
200 | $N_{ld}(t,s,L)$ -- the minimal length of LD $\;s_L$-codes of size~$t$. We also define
201 | a {\em rate} of LD $\;s_L$-codes:
202 |  \beq{RsL}
203 |  R_L(s)\eq \varlimsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t_{ld}(N,s,L)}{N}\,=\,
204 |   \varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t}{N_{ld}(t,s,L)}.
205 |  \eeq
206 | 
207 | If $L=\ell=1$, then Definitions 1 and  2 coincide, i.e., $R_1(s)=R(s,1)$, $s=1,2,\dots$, and
208 | the corresponding code is called a   {\em superimposed $s$-code}. Superimposed $s$-codes
209 | were introduced in the initial paper~\cite{ks64}, where the first nontrivial properties, applications and constructions
210 | of  superimposed $s$-codes were established. In addition, the problem to obtain bounds on the rate~$R(s,1)$
211 | was formulated.

216 | \subsection{Lower and Upper Bounds on  $R(s,1)$}
217 | \quad
218 | Up to now, the best  lower bound on the rate $R(s,1)$ was obtained in paper~\cite{drr89},
219 | where using a random coding method based on the ensemble of binary constant-weight codes,
220 | we proved that
221 | 
222 | \beq{ran1}
223 | R(s,1)\ge\underline{R}(s,1)\eq\,s^{-1}\cdot\,\max\limits_{0<Q<1}\,{A(s,Q)},\qquad s=1,2,\dots,
224 | \eeq
225 | \beq{ran2}
226 |  A(s,Q)\eq\log_2\frac{Q}{1-y}-sK(Q,1-y)-\,K\left(Q,\frac{1-y}{1-y^s}\right),
227 | \eeq
228 | 
229 | \beq{Kul}
230 | K(a,b)\eq a\cdot \log_2 \frac{a}{b}+(1-a)\cdot \log_2 \frac{1-a}{1-b},\quad 0<a,b<1,
231 | \eeq
232 |  and  $y=y(s,Q)$, $1-Q\le y<1$, is the unique root of equation:
233 | \beq{ran3}
234 | y=1\,-\,Q\,+\,Q\,y^s\cdot\frac{1-y}{1-y^s},
235 | \qquad 1-Q\le y<1.
236 | \eeq
237 |

239 | If $s\to\infty$, then the asymptotic behavior  of~\req{ran1}-\req{ran3} has the form:
240 | \beq{ran4}
241 | R(s,1)\,\ge\,\underline{R}(s,1)=\frac{1}{s^2\log_2 e }(1+o(1))=
242 | \frac{0,693}{s^2}(1+o(1)).
243 | \eeq
244 | Here and below  $e=2,718$ is the base of natural logarithm.

249 | upper bound on  $R(s,1)$ was proved in~\cite{dr82}. This upper bound is called a
250 | {\em recurrent bound} and it will be denoted by the symbol $\overline{R}(s,1)$, $s=1,2,\dots$.
251 | For its description, we introduce the standard notation of binary entropy
252 | \beq{entropy}
253 | h(v)\,\eq-v\,\log_2v-(1-v)\log_2(1-v), \qquad 0<v<1,
254 | \eeq
255 | and for each integer $s$, $s\ge1$, define the following function:
256 | \beq{rec1}
257 | f_s(v)\eq h(v/s)-v\, h(1/s),\quad 0<v<1,\quad s=1,2,\dots.
258 | \eeq
259 |  Evidently~\cite{dr82}, for any value of argument  $v$, $0<v<1$,
260 | the function  $f_s(v)$ is positive and $\cap$-convex. In addition,
261 |  its maximal value
262 | \beq{rec1-1}
263 | \max\limits_{0<v<1}\;f_s(v)\,=\,f_s(v_s)\quad\text{where}\quad
264 | v_s\eq\frac{s}{1+2^{s\cdot h\left(\frac{1}{s}\right)}},\quad s=1,2,\dots.
265 | \eeq
266 | Put
267 | \beq{rec2}
268 | \overline{R}(1,1)\eq 1,\quad
269 | \overline{R}(2,1)\eq\max_{0<v<1}\;f_2(v)\,=\,f_2(v_2)\,=\,0.322,
270 | \eeq
271 | and for $s=3,4,\dots$,  the sequence  $\overline{R}(s,1)$ is defined~\cite{dr82} as
272 | the unique root of recurrent equation:
273 | \beq{rec3}
274 | \overline{R}(s,1)=\,f_s\left(1-
275 | \frac{\overline{R}(s,1)}{\overline{R}(s-1,1)}\right),\quad s=3,4,\dots.
276 | \eeq

328 | \subsection{Upper and Lower Bounds on  $ R(s,\ell)$ for  $2\le\ell\le s$}
329 | \quad
330 | Superimposed cover-free $(s,\ell)$-codes (CF $(s,\ell)$-codes)
331 | were introduced in~\cite{mp88}.

333 | The first upper bounds on  $R(s,\ell)$ for CF $(s,\ell)$-codes,
334 | $2\le\ell\le  s$, were obtained in~\cite{dmtv02} and~\cite{s00}.
335 | In papers~\cite{l02}-\cite{l03}, it was proved the recurrent inequality:
336 | \beq{leb}
337 | R(s,\l)\,\le\;
338 | \frac{R(s-i,\l-j)}{R(s-i,\l-j)+\frac{(i+j)^{i+j}}{i^i\cdot j^j}},\quad i\in[s-1],\; j\in[\l-1],
339 | \eeq
340 | which can be  considered as an improvement of the recurrent inequality

342 | R(s,\l)\,\le\, R(s-i,\l-j)\cdot\frac{i^i\cdot j^j}{(i+j)^{i+j}},\quad i\in[s-1],\; j\in[\l-1],
343 | \eeq

349 | \frac{\overline{R}(s-i,\l-j)}{\overline{R}(s-i,\l-j)+\frac{(i+j)^{i+j}}{i^i\cdot j^j}},\quad 2\le\l\le s.

486 | \subsection{Bounds on the rate $R_L(s)$ for LD $\;s_L$-codes}
487 | \quad
488 | Superimposed list-decoding codes (LD $\;s_L$-codes) were introduced in~\cite{dr83} where  nontrivial
489 |  bounds on the rate $R_L(s)$  were obtained. Some constructions were considered in~\cite{dmr00_2}
490 | (see, also~\cite{dmtvy00} and~\cite{v98}) in the connection with two-stage pooling designs arising
491 | in molecular biology to identify any $p$-subset, $p\le s$, of positive  clones in the clone-library of size~$t$.
492 | From Definition~2, it follows the possibility to apply LD $s_L$-code $X$ of size $t$ and length $N$ at the first screening stage.
493 | Then  $\le s+L-1$ candidates are confirmed individually in a confirmatory (second) screening
494 | stage. In other words, if the number of positive clones $\le s$, then the two stage list decoding algorithm
495 | needs to carry out $\le N+s+L-1$ tests (pools). Note, that at fixed  $s\ge2$, the rate of two-stage
496 | pooling designs  $R_L(s)$ is an increasing function of parameter $L\ge1$ and, hence, the number
497 | \beq{limR_L}
498 | R_{\infty}(s)\,\eq\,
499 | \lim\limits_{L\to\infty}\,R_L(s)
500 | \eeq
501 | can be interpreted as a  {\em maximal rate} for two-stage group testing in the disjunct search model
502 | of $p$, $p\le s$, positives.
503 | 
504 | From Definition~2 it follows immediately important properties of СД $\;s_L$-codes
505 |  presented below in the form of Propositions~1-3.
506 | 
507 | \medskip
508 | 
509 | 
510 | \textbf{Proposition 1.}~\cite{dr83}. \quad Let $X$ be an arbitrary
511 | СД $\;s_L$-code of length $N$ and size~$t$.
512 | 
513 | Let  $M_s(\y,X)$, $\y\in\{0,1\}^N$, denote the set of  $s$-collections of
514 | codewords of $X$ such that for any $s$-collection from $M_s(\y,X)$,
515 | the disjunct sum of its $s$ codewords is equal to~$\y$.
516 | Using Definition~2 and arguments by contradiction,  we obtain that for any $\y\in\{0,1\}^N$, the size
517 | $|M_s(\y,X)|\le {s+L-1\choose s}$. Since the number of all  $s$-collections of codewords of $X$
518 | is equal to~${t\choose s}$, then
519 | $$
520 | {t\choose s}\,=
521 | 
522 | \,\sum_{\y}\left|M_s(\y,X)\right|\,
523 | \le\,{s+L-1\choose s}\,2^N.
524 | $$
525 | According to definition~\req{RsL},  these inequalities lead to lower and upper bounds:
526 | 
527 | \beq{lb-triv1}
528 | N_{ld}(t,s,L)\ge\log_2\frac{{t\choose s}}{{s+L-1\choose s}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
529 |  \left(R_L(s)\le \frac{1}{s}\right),\quad s\ge1, \quad L\ge1.
530 | \eeq
531 | 
532 | 
533 | \textbf{Proposition 2.}\quad
534 | Let $s>L\ge2$, and $X$ be an arbitrary LD $\;s_L$-code of length $N$ and size~$t$.
535 | A column (codeword) of  $X$  is called  \emph{bad} for $X$, if  $X$ contains
536 | $\len s/L\rin$ other columns such that their disjunct sum covers the given column.
537 | Otherwise, we   say that the column is \emph{good} and, obviously,  the set of all good
538 | columns is a classical superimposed $\len s/L\rin$-code.
539 | Note that LD $\;s_L$-code $X$ can contain  $\le L-1$ bad columns.
540 | Actually, if there exists an $L$-collection of bad columns of $X$, then the given  $L$-collection
541 | is covered by a disjunct sum of  $\;\le\, L\cdot\len s/L\rin\le s\;$ columns of $X$. This
542 | contradicts to the definition of LD $\;s_L$-code.
543 | In other words, any  $\;s_L$-code of size~$t$  and length~$N$ contains at least  $\; t-(L-1)\;$ codewords
544 | constituting a superimposed  $\len s/L\rin$-code of  length~$N$. Therefore, the maximal size
545 |    $t_{ld}(N,s,L)$ and the rate $R_L(s)$ satisfy inequalities
546 | \beq{lb-triv2}
547 | t_{ld}(N,s,L)\,\le\,t_{ld}\left(N,\len s/L\rin,1\right)+L-1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
548 | R_L(s)\,\le\,R\left(\len s/L\rin,1\right),
549 | \quad L\le s.
550 | \eeq
551 | 
552 | 
553 | \textbf{Proposition 3.}~\cite{dr83}. \quad
554 | {\em If all ${t\choose s}$ disjunct sums corresponding to different  $s$-collections of columns of a code~$X$ are distinct,
555 | then the code $X$ is an LD $\;(s-1)_2$-code}.\quad
556 | The given sufficient condition for LD $\;(s-1)_2$-code is evidently proved by contradiction.

563 | and the upper bound~\req{up-non-rec}. The lower bound on $R_L(s)$ was proved

574 | \textbf{Theorem 6.} \quad (Recurrent upper bound  $\overline{R}_L(s)$).

744 | \subsection{Disjunct Search Designs}
745 | \quad
746 | \textbf{Definition 3.}~\cite{ks64},\cite{dr83}.\quad
747 | Code $X$ is called {\em disjunct $s$-design} ($(\le s)$-{\em design}),
748 | if the disjunct  (Boolean) sum of any collection   $s$ ($\le s$)
749 | columns of code $X$ differs from disjunct sum of any other collection
750 | containing  $s$  ($\le s$) columns of code~$X$.
751 | Denote by $N(t,=s)$ ($N(t,\le s)$) the minimal number of rows for disjunct  $s$-design ($(\le s)$-design)
752 | of size $t$ and define a {\em rate} of disjunct  $s$-designs ($(\le s)$-designs):
753 |  \beq{Rdes}
754 |  R(=s)\eq\varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t}{N(t,=s)},\qquad
755 |  R(\le s)\eq\varlimsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\log_2 t}{N(t,\le s)} .
756 |  \eeq
757 | 
758 | Obviously~\cite{ks64}, the rate
759 | \beq{Rdes-triv}
760 | R(\le s)\,\le\, R(= s)\le 1/s,\qquad s=1,2,\dots.
761 | \eeq
762 | In the non-adaptive disjunct search model of $s$ $\;(\le s)$ defects
763 | among a set of  $t$ elements, Definition~3 gives the necessary and sufficient condition
764 | for identification. Any disjunct $s$-design can be considered as the incidence matrix
765 | for a {\em union-free family}~\cite{cop98} containing  $t$ subset of the  set~$[N]$.
766 | For rates~\req{Rsl}-\req{RsL} and~\req{Rdes}, the following inequalities
767 | \beq{Rdes-b}
768 | R(s,1)\,\le R(\le s)\,\le\,R(s-1,1), \qquad
769 |  R(=s)\,\le\, R_2(s-1),\qquad s=2,3,\dots,
770 |  \eeq
771 | hold. The first two inequalities were observed in~\cite{ks64} and the third inequality
772 | was established in~\cite{dr83} as the consequence of Proposition~3.

807 | \quad
808 | Let $s=2,3,\dots$  be a fixed integer and $f_s(x)$ is a function of $x$,  $0<x<1$,
809 | defined by ~\req{entropy}-\req{rec1}, and
810 | $$
811 | K_s\,\eq\,\left[\max_{0\leq x\leq\frac{K_s-K_{s-1}}{K_s}}f_s(x)\right]^{-1}, \quad s=2,3,\dots ,\quad K_1 \eq 1,
812 | $$
813 | denotes the recurrent sequence, introduced in~\cite{dr82}. Then the Statement of Theorem 1, i.e., the inequality~\req{low-non-rec}, is equivalent to:
814 | \beq{upKs}
815 | K_s\,\le\,\frac{(s+1)^2}{2\log_2[(s+1)/8]},\qquad s\ge8.
816 | \eeq
817 | 
818 | For $9\leq s\leq 236$, the correctness of~\req{upKs} was checked with the help of computer calculations.
819 | For $s\ge237$, the proof of~\req{upKs} is based on the following properties of the sequence $K_s$:
820 | \beq{K1}
821 | K_s = \left[f_s\left(1 - \frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}\right)\right]^{-1},\quad
822 | 1-\frac{K_{s - 1}}{K_s}\,<\, v_s,
823 | \quad s\ge3;
824 | \eeq
825 | \beq{K2}
826 | v_s\,> \,\frac{s}{1+se}\,> \,\frac{2}{s},\qquad s\ge8.
827 | \eeq
828 | Definitions~\req{entropy}-\req{rec2} were used in previous formulas. For the fixed parameter $s\ge3$, Statement~\req{K1} and first inequality in~\req{K2}
829 | were established in~\cite{dr82} and~\cite{dmtv02}. For $s\ge8$, the second inequality in~\req{K2} is evident. Moreover, for $0<x<1$ and $s\ge2$, the following bounds
830 | \begin{multline}\label{low-f}
831 | f_s(x) \eq -\frac{x}{s}\left(\log_2 x - \log_2 s\right) - \left(1 - \frac{x}{s}\right) \log_2\left[1-\frac{x}{s}\right] - \frac{x}{s}\log_2 s + x\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right]=\\
832 | =-\frac{x}{s}\log_2 x + x\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right] - \left(1-\frac{x}{s}\right)\log_2\left[1-\frac{x}{s}\right]\geq\\
833 | \geq-\frac{x}{s}\log_2 x + x\left(1-\frac{1}{s}\right)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right] + \left(1-\frac{x}{s}\right)\frac{x}{s}\log_2 e,
834 | \quad 0<x<1,\; s\ge2,
835 | \end{multline}
836 | and
837 | \beq{log2e}
838 | \left(s-1\right)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right]>-\log_2 e,\quad s\ge2,
839 | \eeq
840 | hold,  which were obtained from definitions~\req{entropy}-\req{rec1} of the function $f_s(x)$  and
841 | ordinary logarithmic inequality
842 | \beq{ln}
843 | \ln u\,\le\, u-1, \qquad u>0.
844 | \eeq
845 | 
846 | To prove the inequality~\req{upKs} for $s\ge237$ we consider two distinct cases. In the first case it is assumed that $1 - \frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}>\frac{2}{s},\, s\ge237$, and in the second case -- that
847 | $1 - \frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}\le\frac{2}{s},\,s\ge237$.
848 | \begin{enumerate}
849 | \item
850 | Let $s\ge237$ and $1 - \frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}>\frac{2}{s}$.  Then, applying the property of monotonous increase of function $f_s(x)$, $0<x\le v_s$,
851 | and~\req{K1}-\req{log2e},
852 | it is easy to check the following Statements:
853 | \begin{multline*}
854 | K_s=\frac{1}{f_s\left(1-\frac{K_{s - 1}}{K_s}\right)}<\frac{1}{{f_s}\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)}
855 | \leq\frac{1}{\frac{2}{s^2}\log_2\left[\frac{s}{2}\right]+\frac{2\log_2 e}{s^2}\left(1-\frac{2}{s^2}\right)+\frac{2(s-1)}{s^2}\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right]}=\\
856 | =\frac{s^2}{2\log_2\left[\frac{s}{2}\right]+2\log_2 e-\frac{4\log_2 e}{s^2}+2(s-1)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right]}<\frac{s^2}{2\log_2\left[\frac{s}{4}\right]}.
857 | \end{multline*}
858 | This yields inequality~\req{upKs} for the given particular case.
859 | \item
860 | Suppose $s\ge237$ and $1 - \frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}\leq\frac{2}{s}$. Introduce variable $t_s\eq 1-\frac{K_{s-1}}{K_s}\le\frac{2}{s}$.
861 | Using~\req{K1}-\req{low-f}, we can write
862 | \beq{K3}
863 | K_s = \frac{1}{f_s(t_s)}\leq\frac{1}{-\frac{t_s}{s}\log_2 t_s + \frac{t_s}{s}\left(1-\frac{t_s}{s}\right)\log_2 e+t_s\frac{s-1}{s}\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\right]}=\frac{s}{T_s(t_s)t_s},
864 | \eeq
865 | where for any $x>0$ the function
866 | $$
867 | T_s(x)\eq -\log_2x + \log_2e-\frac{x\log_2e}{s}+(s-1)\log_2\left[1-\frac{1}{s}\]
868 | \geq -\frac{x\log_2e}{s}-\log_2 x\eq q_s(x),
869 | $$
870 | and lower bound for $T_s(x)$ is implied by~\req{log2e}.
871 | It is easy to see that the function $q_s(x)$ is decreasing for $x>0$. Since $t_s\leq\frac{2}{s}$ we obtain $q_s(t_s)\geq q_s\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)$.
872 | 
873 | This leads to
874 | \beq{K4}
875 | T_s(t_s)\ge q_s(t_s)\ge q_s\left(\frac{2}{s}\right)= -\frac{2\log_2 e}{s^2}+\log_2\left[\frac{s}{2}\right]>0.
876 | \eeq
877 | Using ~\req{K3} and ~\req{K4}, we get
878 | \beq{K5}
879 | K_s - K_{s-1}=K_s t_s\le\frac{s}{T_s}
880 | \leq\frac{s}{\log_2\left[\frac{s}{2}\right]-\frac{2\log_2 e}{s^2}}
881 | \leq \frac{s}{\log_2\left[\frac{s}{4}\right]}, \quad s\ge8,
882 | \eeq
883 | i.e., the recurrent inequality
884 | \beq{KsNew}
885 | K_s\,\le\,K_{s-1}\,+\, \frac{s}{\log_2\left[\frac{s}{4}\right]}, \quad s\ge8,
886 | \eeq
887 | holds.
888 | If the following inequality
889 | \beq{K6}
890 | \frac{s^2}{2\log_2\left[\frac{s}{8}\right]}+\frac{s}{\log_2\left[\frac{s}{4}\right]}<
891 | \frac{(s+1)^2}{2\log_2\left[\frac{s+1}{8}\right]}, \quad s\ge237
892 | \eeq
893 | holds, then for the considered case the inequality~\req{KsNew} yields~\req{upKs} for any $s\ge237$.
894 | Taking into account the logarithmic property~\req{ln}, inequality~\req{K6} could be obtained from
895 | \beq{K7}
896 | \frac{s^2}{2\log_2\left[\frac{s}{8}\right]}+\frac{s}{\log_2\left[\frac{s}{4}\right]}<
897 | \frac{(s+1)^2}{2\(\log_2s + \frac{\log_2e}{s} - 3\)}, \quad s\ge237.
898 | \eeq
899 | It is easy to check that the inequality~\req{K7} is equivalent to the following one:
900 | $$
901 | s\left((2-\log_2 e)\log_2 s + 2\log_2 e - 6\right) + \log_2^2 s - (5+2\log_2 e)\log_2 s + 6 + 6\log_2 e > 0,
902 | $$
903 | which could be verified evidently for any $s \ge 237$ .
904 | \end{enumerate}
905 | 
906 | Theorem 1 is proved.$\quad \square$

912 | \quad
913 | \textbf{Proof of Statement 1.}\quad
914 | To obtain the random coding (lower) bound on $R(s,\l)$, for $\l\ge2$, we use the ensemble of binary constant-weight codes, suggested in~\cite{drr89}.
915 | Let $Q$, $0<Q<1$, and $\ell$,  $2\le\ell\le s$, $s+\ell<t$ be fixed parameters. Using notations~\req{X}, we introduce $E(N,t,Q)$ --
916 | the ensemble of binary $( N\times t)$-matrices $X$  with
917 | $N$ rows and $t$ columns, where each column is chosen independently from the set of columns with fixed weight $w\eq\lfloor QN\rfloor$.
918 | A pair $\p_{s, \ell}$ of sets of nonordered columns
919 | $$
920 | \Big\{\x(1),\x(2),\dots,
921 | \x(s)\Big\},\quad \Big\{\x(s + 1), \dots\x(s+\ell)\Big\}
922 | $$
923 | is called a $(s,\ell)$-{\em bad} pair if there is no row $\x_i$, $i\in [N]$, in which
924 | $$
925 | x_i(j)=0 \quad \text{for any }j\in[s],\quad x_i(k)=1\quad \text{for any }k\in[s+\ell]\setminus[s].
926 | $$
927 | By~$P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)$ denote a probability of such event in the ensemble $E(N,t,Q)$.
928 | A column is called $(s,\ell)$-{\em bad} if there exists a pair $\p_{s, \ell}$ of sets consisting of different columns and the column belongs to the second set.
929 | Let $P_1=P_1(N,t,Q,s,\ell)$ denote the probability of such event in the ensemble~$E(N,t,Q)$.
930 | From the union inequality it follows that
931 | $$
932 | P_1\le {{t-1} \choose {s+\ell-1}} {{s+\ell-1} \choose {s}}
933 | P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)\,\le\,\frac{t^{s+\ell-1}}{s!(\ell-1)!}\,P_0(N,Q,s,\ell).
934 | $$
935 | This implies that the average number of $(s,\ell)$-bad columns does not exceed
936 | $$
937 | t\,P_1\,<\,t\, \frac{t^{s+\ell-1}}{s!(\ell-1)!}\,P_0(N,Q,s,\ell).
938 | $$
939 | Therefore, for any
940 | $$
941 | t\,<\,\left[\frac{s!(\ell-1)!}{2\,P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)}\right]^{1/(s+\ell-1)}
942 | $$
943 | there exists an $(N\times t/2)$-matrix $X$ which is a CF $(s,\ell)$-code. Thus, the maximum size
944 | $$
945 | t_{cf}(N,s,\ell)\,\ge\,\left\lfloor\,\frac12\,\left[\frac{s!(\ell-1)!}
946 | {2\,P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)}\right]^{1/(s+\ell-1)}\right\rfloor, \quad 0<Q<1.
947 | $$
948 | Taking into account definition~\req{Rsl}, we get inequality
949 | \beq{First}
950 | R(s,\ell)\ge
951 | \underline{R}(s,\l)\eq
952 | \frac{1}{s+\ell-1}\,\max_{0<Q<1}A(s,\ell,Q), \qquad 2\le\ell\le s,
953 | $$
954 | $$
955 | A(s,\ell,Q)\eq\overline{\lim_{N \to \infty}}\frac{-\log_2 P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)}{N}, \quad 0<Q<1.
956 | \eeq
957 | To complete the proof of Statement 1 one need to obtain an explicit formula for the function
958 | $A(s,\ell,Q)$ and to show that the right-hand side of~\req{First} is equal to~\req{Th4}.
959 | 
960 | 
961 | We will use the type terminology~\cite{ck85}. Consider two sets of columns
962 | $\x(1),\dots,\x(s)$ and~$\y(1)\dots,\y(\ell)$, having the fixed weight $w$. The first set forms
963 | $(N\times s)$-matrix $X_0$, and the second one -- $(N\times
964 | \ell)$-matrix~$Y_0$. Let $\x\in\{0,\,1\}^s$ and $\y\in\{0,\,1\}^\ell$. By $\{n(\x)\}$ $\left(\{m(\y)\}\right)$ denote the type of the matrix $X_0$ $\(Y_0\)$.
965 | This means that $\{n(\x)\}$ $\left(\{m(\y)\}\right)$ is a number of rows $\x$ ($\y$) in the matrix $X_0$ $\(Y_0\)$. By $n\left(\0\right)$ $\(m\left(\1\right)\)$ denote a number of rows of all zeros (ones) in $X_0$ $\(Y_0\)$.
966 | 
967 | 
968 | Let $\x(1),\dots,\x(s)$ and $\y(1)\dots,\y(\ell)$ be columns with fixed types $\{n(\x)\}$ and $\{m(\y)\}$ respectively. Let $G(\{n(\x)\},\{m(\y)\})$ be a number of $(s,\ell)$-bad pairs of sets.
969 | It is easy to check that $G(\{n(\x)\},\{m(\y)\})$ equals
970 | \beq{Gnm}
971 | \frac{N!}{\prod_{\x}n(\x)!}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\y}m(\y)!}
972 | \frac{{ {N-n\left(\0\right)} \choose m\left(\1\right)}}{{ {N} \choose m\left(\1\right)}}.
973 | \eeq
974 | The function~\req{Gnm} is well defined because a number of matrices $\(X_0,\,Y_0\)$ with fixed type equals $\frac{N!}{\prod_{\x}n(\x)!}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\y}m(\y)!}$ and a relative number of
975 | $(s,\ell)$-bad pairs of sets is equal to $\frac{{
976 | {N-n\left(\0\right)} \choose m\left(\1\right)}}{{ {N} \choose
977 | m\left(\1\right)}}$. Note that if $N - n(\0) < m(\1)$ holds, then a pair $\p_{s, \ell}$ is bad.
978 | 
979 | From~\req{Gnm} it follows that the probability
980 | \begin{multline} \label{Pr0}
981 | P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)=\sum_{\{n(\x)\}}\sum_{\{m(\y)\}}G(\{n(\x)\},\{m(\y)\}){N \choose w}^{-s-\ell}=\\
982 | =\sum_{\{n(\x)\}}\sum_{\{m(\y)\}}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\x}n(\x)!}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\y}m(\y)!}
983 | \frac{{ {N-n\left(\0\right)} \choose m\left(\1\right)}}{{ {N} \choose m\left(\1\right)}} {N \choose w}^{-s-\ell},
984 | \end{multline}
985 | where the sum is taken over all such types $\{n(\x)\}$,$\{m(\y)\}$, satisfying the following conditions:
986 | \beq{condD}
987 | \begin{cases}
988 | n(\0)+m(\1)\le N;\\
989 | 0\le n(\x)\le N;\quad 0\le m(\y)\le N;\\
990 | \sum\limits_{\x}n(\x)=\sum_{\y}m(\y)=N;\\
991 | \sum\limits_{\x:\,x_i=1}n(\x)=\sum\limits_{\y:\,y_j=1}m(\y)=QN=w\quad\text{for any } i\in[s],\,j\in[\ell].\\
992 | \end{cases}
993 | \eeq
994 | 
995 | Let $N \to \infty$ and
996 | $ n(\x)\eq N[\tau(\x)+o(1)]$,  $m(\y)\eq N[\chi(\y)+o(1)]$, where probability distributions $\tau\eq\{\tau(\x)\},\,\x\in\{0,1\}^s$ and
997 |  $\chi\eq\{\chi(\y)\},\,\y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}$ are induced by~\req{condD}, i.e.,
998 | \beq{CondN}
999 | \begin{cases}
1000 | \sum\limits_{\x\in\{0,1\}^s} \tau(\x) = 1,\quad \sum\limits_{\y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}} \chi(\y) = 1,\quad \tau(\0)+\chi(\1)\le1,\\
1001 |  \sum\limits_{\x:\,x_i = 1} \tau(\x) = Q,\quad \sum\limits_{\y:\,y_j=1}\chi(\y) = Q\quad\text{for any }  i\in[s],\,j\in[\ell].\\
1002 |  \end{cases}
1003 | \eeq
1004 | Applying Stirling's approximation for $\{n(\x)\},\{m(\y)\}$ corresponding to these distributions, one can find the logarithmic asimptotics of summand in~\req{Pr0}:
1005 | $$
1006 | -\log_2\left\{ \frac{N!}{\prod_{\x}n(\x)!}\frac{N!}{\prod_{\y}m(\y)!}
1007 | \frac{{ {N-n\left(\0\right)} \choose m\left(\1\right)}}{{ {N} \choose m\left(\1\right)}} {N \choose w}^{-s-\ell}\right\}=N[F(\tau,\chi,Q)+o(1)],
1008 | $$
1009 | where
1010 | \begin{multline*}
1011 | F=F(\tau,\chi,Q)\eq
1012 | \sum_{\x} \tau(\x )\log_2\[\tau(\x )\]
1013 | +\sum_{\y} \chi(\y )\log_2\[\chi(\y )\]
1014 | -(1-\tau\left(\0\right))h\left(\frac{\chi\left(\1\right)}{1-\tau\left(\0\right)}\right)+\\
1015 | +(s+\ell)h(Q)+h(\chi\left(\1\right)).
1016 | \end{multline*}
1017 | 
1018 | Let $\tau_Q=\{\tau_Q(\x)\}$ and $\chi_Q=\{\chi_Q(\x)\}$ be fixed distributions with properties~\req{CondN}, at which the minimum of the function $F(\tau,\chi, Q)$ is attained. Hence, the logarithmic asimptotics of~\req{Pr0} is equal to
1019 | \beq{Second}
1020 | A(s,\ell,Q)\eq\overline{\lim_{N \to \infty}}\frac{-\log_2 P_0(N,Q,s,\ell)}{N}=
1021 | \min\limits_{\tau,\,\chi} F(\tau,\chi,Q)=F(\tau_Q,\chi_Q,Q),
1022 | \eeq
1023 | where the minimum is taken over all distributions provided that~\req{CondN}.
1024 | 
1025 | It is sufficient to find the minimum of the function $F(\tau,\chi,Q)$ over the same region~\req{CondN}, but without bounds.
1026 | Thus, we investigate the problem of minimization:
1027 | $$
1028 | F\to \min.
1029 | $$
1030 | \begin{align}\label{Pro}
1031 | &\text{Main function:}\quad\quad F(\tau,\chi,Q): \mathbb{X}\to \mathbb{R}.& \notag\\
1032 | &\text{Restrictions:}\,\,
1033 | \begin{cases}
1034 |  \sum\limits_{\x\in\{0,1\}^s} \tau(\x) = 1;\\
1035 |  \sum\limits_{\y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}} \chi(\y) = 1;\\
1036 |  \sum\limits_{\x:\,x_i = 1} \tau(\x) = Q\quad\text{for any }  i\in[s];\\
1037 |  \sum\limits_{\y:\,y_j=1}\chi(\y) = Q\quad \text{for any }  j\in[\ell].
1038 |  \end{cases}&\\
1039 | &\text{Search region }\mathbb{X}:\,\,
1040 | \begin{cases}
1041 | 0<\tau(\x)<1\quad \text{for any }  \x\in\{0,1\}^s;\\
1042 | 0<\chi(\y)<1\quad \text{for any } \y\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}; \notag\\
1043 | \tau(\0)+\chi(\1)<1.
1044 | \end{cases}&
1045 | \end{align}

1047 | We look for the extremal distribution $(\tau_Q,\chi_Q)$ with the help of Lagrange's method. Lagrangian is written as
1048 | \begin{multline*}
1049 | \Lambda \eq F(\tau,\chi, Q) + \lambda_0 \left(\sum_{\x} \tau(\x) - 1\right) + \lambda_1 \left(\sum_{\y} \chi(\y) - 1\right)+\\
1050 | +\sum_{i = 1}^s \mu_i \left(\sum_{\x:\,x_i = 1} \tau(\x) - Q\right)+\sum_{i = 1}^{\ell} \nu_i \left(\sum_{\y:\,y_i = 1} \chi(\y) - Q\right).
1051 | \end{multline*}
1052 | Necessary conditions for $(\tau_Q,\chi_Q)$ have the following form:
1053 | \beq {Cond}
1054 | \begin{cases}
1055 | \frac{\partial \Lambda}{\partial(\tau(\x))} = \log_2 \[\tau(\x)\] + \log_2 e + \lambda_0 + \sum\limits_{i:\,x_i = 1} \mu_i\,=\,0 \quad \text{for any }  \x \neq \0; \\
1056 | \frac{\partial \Lambda}{\partial(\tau(\0)} = \log_2 \[\tau(\0)\] + \log_2 e + \lambda_0 + \log_2\[\frac{1-\tau\left(\0\right)}{1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\(\1\)}\]=0;\\
1057 | \frac{\partial \Lambda}{\partial(\chi(\y))}=\log_2\[ \chi(\y)\] + \log_2 e +\lambda_1 + \sum\limits_{i:\,y_i = 1}\nu_i\,=\,0\quad \text{for any } \y \neq \1; \\
1058 | \frac{\partial \Lambda}{\partial(\chi\left(\1\right))}=\log_2 \[\chi\left(\1\right)\] + \log_2 e +\lambda_1 + \sum\limits_{i= 1}^{\ell}\nu_i+\log_2\[\frac{1-\chi\left(\1\right)}{1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right)}\]\,=\,0.\\
1059 | \end{cases}
1060 | \eeq

1062 | \textbf{Remark 3.}\quad
1063 | Note that inequality $\ell\ge2$ is significant for~\req {Cond},
1064 | since at $\ell=L=1$ variables $\chi\left(\1\right)\equiv Q$  and $\chi\left(\0\right)\equiv1-Q$
1065 | are constant. Hence, for the case $L=1$ the similar system~\req {Cond3} does not contain two last equations of~\req {Cond}.
1066 | Hense, two cases ($\l=1$ and $\l\ge2$) will lead to different asymptotic bounds.
1067 | 
1068 | The Hessian matrix of Lagrangian is
1069 | \begin{align*}
1070 | &\frac{\partial^2 \Lambda}{\partial^2(\tau(\x))}=\frac{\log_2 e}{\tau(\x)}>0\quad \text{for any }  \x\neq\0;\\
1071 | &\frac{\partial^2 \Lambda}{\partial^2(\chi(\y))}=\frac{\log_2 e}{\chi(\y)}>0\quad \text{for any } \y\neq\1;\\
1072 | &a'\eq\frac{\partial^2 \Lambda}{\partial(\tau\left(\0\right))^2}=\frac{\log_2 e}{\tau\left(\0\right)}+\frac{\log_2 e\cdot\chi\left(\1\right)}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}>0;\\
1073 | &b'\eq\frac{\partial^2 \Lambda}{\partial(\tau\left(\0\right))\partial(\chi\left(\1\right))}=\frac{\log_2 e}{1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right)}>0;\\
1074 | &c'\eq\frac{\partial^2 \Lambda}{\partial(\chi\left(\1\right))^2}=\frac{\log_2 e}{\chi\left(\1\right)}+\frac{\log_2 e\cdot\tau\left(\0\right)}{(1-\chi\left(\1\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}>0.
1075 | \end{align*}
1076 | To prove that the Hessian matrix is positive definite it is sufficient to check~$a'c'-{b'}^2>0$.
1077 | We have
1078 | \begin{multline*}
1079 | \frac{a'c'-{b'}^2}{(\log_2 e)^2}=\frac{1}{\tau\left(\0\right)\chi\left(\1\right)}+\frac{1}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}+
1080 | \frac{1}{(1-\chi(\left(\1\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}+\\
1081 | +\frac{\tau\left(\0\right)\chi\left(\1\right)}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right))(1-\chi\left(\1\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))^2}-\frac{1}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))^2}=\\
1082 | =\frac{1}{\tau\left(\0\right)\chi\left(\1\right)}+\frac{1}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}+
1083 | \frac{1}{(1-\chi\left(\1\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}-\\
1084 | -\frac{1}{(1-\tau\left(\0\right))(1-\chi\left(\1\right))(1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right))}\ge \frac{1-\tau\left(\0\right)\chi\left(\1\right)}{1-\tau\left(\0\right)-\chi\left(\1\right)}>0.
1085 | \end{multline*}
1086 | Note that the function $F$ is strictly convex on $\mathbb{X}$ because it has the same Hessian matrix as Lagrangian.
~~~~

## 4. Exact released witness

~~~~text
Take s=4, L=2, so floor(s/L)=2. Represent six binary columns by supports: A={1,2}, B={1,3}, C={2,3}, D={4}, E={5}, F={6}. Then A⊆B∪C, B⊆A∪C, and C⊆A∪B. If an omitted column is D, E or F, its private coordinate is absent from the selected columns, so that column is not covered. If both omitted columns lie among A,B,C, only one of that triple is selected, and its support covers neither omitted column. Thus at most one outside column is covered by any four-column selection, as required for L=2.
~~~~
