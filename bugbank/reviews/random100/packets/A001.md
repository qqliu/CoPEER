304 | \begin{prop}[Strands of 2-ended $\cato$ spaces]\label{prop:strands}
305 | 	Let $X$ be a 2-ended $\cato$ metric space and let $\Gamma$ be a
306 | 	group that acts properly and cocompactly on $X$ by isometries. There
307 | 	is a canonical geodesic $\sigma:\bbR \to X$ called the
308 | 	\define{$\Gamma$-strand} of $X$ with the property that
309 | 	$\Gamma \cdot \sigma = \sigma$. Furthermore $\sigma$ is an axis of
310 | 	translation for every hyperbolic isometry of $X$.
311 | \end{prop}

318 | \begin{defn}[Types of 2-ended groups]\label{defn:2e-types}
319 | 	Let $\Gamma$ be 2-ended group, then either it maps onto $\bbZ$, in
320 | 	which case we say it is of \define{$\bbZ$-type} or it maps onto $D_\infty = \bbZ \rtimes
321 | 	\bbZ/2\bbZ$ in which case we say it is of \define{dihedral type}. If
322 | 	$\Gamma$ is of dihedral type, we denote by $\Gamma^+$ the preimage of
323 | 	the maximal infinite cyclic subgroup of $D_\infty$, otherwise let $\Gamma^+=\Gamma$.

347 | \begin{thm}[{see \cite[Theorem II.6.8]{bridson2011metric}}]\label{thm:bh-isom}

366 | \begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:strands}]
367 | 	We start by considering $\Gamma^+$. Let $\pi$ be canonical the
368 | 	surjection of $\Gamma$ onto $\bbZ$ or $\bbZ\rtimes \bbZ/2\bbZ$, and
369 | 	let $K = \ker(\pi)$. Let $g \in \Gamma^+$ be an element that maps to
370 | 	a generator of the maximal infinite cyclic subgroup. $K$ is finite
371 | 	and we have $\Gamma^+=\bk{g,K}$. Conjugation by $g$ induces an
372 | 	automorphism of the finite group $K$ so if $n$ is the exponent of
373 | 	$\Aut K$ then $g^n$ commutes with every element in $K$. Since $g^n$
374 | 	commutes with $g$ as well we conclude that $g^n$ is central in
375 | 	$\Gamma^+$. It follows from Theorem \ref{thm:bh-isom}
376 | 	(\ref{it:things-that-commute}) that $\Gamma^+ = \bk{g,K}$ leaves
377 | 	$\mathrm{Min}(g^n) = Y\times \bbR$ invariant.
378 | 
379 | 	Again, by Theorem \ref{thm:bh-isom} (\ref{it:things-that-commute}),
380 | 	every $\gamma \in \Gamma^+$ projects to an isometry $\alpha'_\gamma$
381 | 	of $Y$, which itself is a $\cato$ metric space. Since $X$ is 2-ended
382 | 	and $Y\times \bbR$ is a convex subset of $X$, $Y$ must be
383 | 	bounded. Let $\Gamma'=\bk{\alpha'_\gamma \mid \gamma\in \Gamma^+}$
384 | 	and $Y_0\subset Y$ be the set of points fixed pointwise by all
385 | 	elements of $\Gamma'$. Proposition \ref{prop:cato-centre} implies
386 | 	that $Y$ has a centre $c_Y$ which must be fixed by $\Gamma'$ so
387 | 	$Y_0$ is nonempty.
388 | 
389 | 	\emph{Claim: The subset $Y_0 \times \bbR \subset X$ is independent
390 | 		of the choice of $g$.} Let $g'$ be another lift of the image of
391 | 	$g$. Then $(g')^n = g^nk$ for some $k \in K$. $(g')^n$ maps
392 | 	$\mathrm{Min}(g^n)$ to itself, but it may permute axes. Let $(g')^n$
393 | 	act on $Y\times \bbR$ as $(\beta',\beta'')$ where $\beta''$ must be
394 | 	translation by $|g^n|$. By definition of $Y_0$,
395 | 	$Y_0\times \bbR \subset \mathrm{Min}\left((g')^n\right)$ since
396 | 	$\beta'$ acts trivially on $Y_0$. On the other hand if we repeat the
397 | 	construction with $(g')^n$ to get
398 | 	$Y'_0\times \bbR \subset \mathrm{Min}\left((g')^n\right)$ we see
399 | 	that every element of $Y'_0 \times \bbR$ must lie
400 | 	$\mathrm{Min}(g^n)$ and in fact that
401 | 	$Y'_0 \times \bbR \subset Y_0\times \bbR$, so by symmetry both sets
402 | 	are equal. This completes the claim.
403 | 
404 | 	If $\Gamma = \Gamma^+$, i.e. $\Gamma$ is of $\bbZ$-type, then we
405 | 	take $\sigma = \{c_{Y_0}\}\times\bbR \subset Y_0\times \bbR$, where
406 | 	$c_{Y_0}$ is the centre of $Y_0$ in the sense of Proposition
407 | 	\ref{prop:cato-centre}, and we are done. We now turn to the dihedral
408 | 	case.  Let $r \in \Gamma$ be such that $\Gamma = \bk{\Gamma^+,r}$,
409 | 	that is to say that $\pi(r) = \bar r$ is a reflection in
410 | 	$D_\infty$. Let $x \in Y_0\times \bbR$ and let
411 | 	$\rho_x = \{p\} \times \bbR$ be the $g^n$-axis containing $x$.
412 | 
413 | 	\emph{Claim: $r\cdot \rho_x \subset \mathrm{Min}(g^n)$.} We need to
414 | 	show that $g^n$ translates $r\cdot x$ minimally. Note that
415 | 	conjugation by $r$ gives an inverse modulo $k$ of $g^n$. Consider
416 | 	the following:
417 | 	\begin{align*}
418 | 	g^n r \cdot x & = r (r^{-1}g^n r) \cdot x\\
419 | 	& = r g^{-n} k \cdot x\\
420 | 	& = r g^{-n} \cdot x
421 | 	\end{align*}
422 | 	This means that $g^n$ sends the point $r\cdot x \in r \cdot \rho_x$
423 | 	to $r g^{-n} \cdot x \in r \cdot \rho_x$. In particular
424 | 	$d(r\cdot x,g^{n}r\cdot x) = d(x,g^{-n}\cdot x)$, which means that
425 | 	$r\cdot x$ realizes the translation length of $g^n$. This proves the
426 | 	claim.

434 | 	$\Gamma^+$-invariant and therefore in $Y_0\times \bbR$; thus

439 | 	that is fixed pointwise by the image of $\Gamma$. By Proposition

441 | 	and the strand $\sigma = \{c_{Y_1}\}\times \bbR \subset X$ is
442 | 	canonical and $\Gamma$-invariant.

445 | We now classify strands by their ambient stabilizer groups.

447 | \begin{lem}\label{lem:strand-classification}
448 | 	Let $\strand \subset \waffle$ be a strand corresponding to a
449 | 	hyperplane in a waffle $\waffle$, and let $K\leq
450 | 	\Isom\waffle$ act cocompactly on $\waffle$. Then the stabilizer
451 | 	$K_\strand$ is one of the following types:
452 | 	\begin{enumerate}
453 | 		\item $\bbZ$-type,
454 | 		\item dihedral type, or
455 | 		\item\label{it:reflective} isomorphic to $\bbZ \oplus \bbZ_2$ or
456 | 		$(\bbZ\semidirect\bbZ_2) \oplus \bbZ_2$.
457 | 	\end{enumerate}
458 | 	In the latter case the $\bbZ_2$ factor fixes $\sigma$ pointwise.
459 | \end{lem}
460 | \begin{proof}
461 | 	If $K_\strand$ acts freely on $\strand$, then it is of $\bbZ$-type.
462 | 	Let $\strand^\pm$ be the endpoints of $\strand \in \partial\waffle$
463 | 	and suppose that some elements of $K_\strand$ fix a point $x$ of
464 | 	$\strand.$ Then any such elements induce isometries of the circle
465 | 	$\partial\waffle$ with respect to the visual metric based at $x$ and
466 | 	permute $\strand^\pm$ or the connected components of the complement
467 | 	$\partial \waffle\setminus\left\{\strand^\pm\right\}$. Because
468 | 	elements of $K$ are fully determined by their action on
469 | 	$\partial\waffle$, it follows that
470 | 	$(K_\strand)_x \leq \bbZ_2 \oplus \bbZ_2$, so it either has
471 | 	cardinality 1,2 or 4.
472 | 
473 | 	Now $K_\sigma$ acts discretely cocompactly on $\strand$ by
474 | 	isometries, therefore the quotient $K_\strand\backslash\strand$ is
475 | 	either a circle or a closed segment. If the quotient is a circle,
476 | 	then any element that fixes some point in $\strand$, must fix
477 | 	$\strand$ pointwise, so $K_\strand \approx \bbZ \oplus
478 | 	\bbZ_2$. Otherwise there is some $K_x \leq K_\strand$ that permutes
479 | 	the endpoints $\strand^\pm$. If $|K_x|=4$ then there must also be
480 | 	some element $k$ that fixes $\strand^\pm$ and therefore $\strand$
481 | 	pointwise, but permutes the connected components of
482 | 	$\partial\waffle\setminus\{\strand^\pm\}$, so that $K_\strand =
483 | 	(\bbZ\semidirect\bbZ_2) \oplus \bbZ_2$. If $|K_x|=2$ then $K_\strand
484 | 	= \bbZ\semidirect\bbZ_2$.
485 | \end{proof}

544 | \begin{defn}[Horostrip]\label{defn:horostrip}
545 |   Consider the upper half plane model of $\bbH^2$, the hyperbolic
546 |   2-space. For $0<y_1<y_2$ the \define{horostrip} $\panel(y_1,y_2)$ is
547 |   the closed region bounded by the
548 |   lines\[ y= y_1 \textrm{~and~} y=y_2.\] The lines in
549 |   $\panel(y_1,y_2)$ parallel to the $y$-axis are called
550 |   \define{vertical leaves.} The quantity $|\ln(y_2/y_1)|$ is called
551 |   the \define{width} of $\panel(y_1,y_2).$ The boundary component
552 |   contained in $\{(x,y)\mid y = y_1\}$ is called the \define{long side}.

571 | \begin{prop}\label{prop:quadrilaterals}
572 |   Consider a quadrilateral $Q$ contained in the horostrip
573 |   $\panel(y_1,y_2)$ whose boundary is given by the
574 |   union\[ \alpha \cup \ell_1 \cup \beta \cup \ell_2
575 |   \] where $\alpha, \beta \subset \partial \panel(y_1,y_2)$ and
576 |   $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are vertical leaves. Then, with respect to the
577 |   metric induced by $\bbH^2$, $\ell_1,\ell_2$ are geodesics with
578 |   length\[
579 |     \left|\ln\left(\frac{\mathrm{length}(\alpha)}{\mathrm{length}(\beta)}\right)\right|
580 |     = \mathrm{width}(\panel(y_1,y_2)).\]
581 | \end{prop}
582 | \begin{proof}
583 |   If $\gamma(t) = (x(t),y(t))$ is a parametric curve
584 |   $\gamma: [a,b] \to \{(x,y) \in \bbR^2 \mid y>0\}$ and
585 |   $\gamma = \gamma([a,b])$ is a path in $\bbH^2$, then its length is
586 |   given by
587 |   \[ \mathrm{length}(\gamma) = \int_\gamma ds_{\bbH^2}=
588 |     \int_a^b\frac{\sqrt{x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2}}{y(t)}dt.\] Without loss of
589 |   generality $\alpha \subset \{(x,y) \mid y=y_1\}$ and $y_1<y_2$. The result now
590 |   follows from the paramaterizations:
591 |   \begin{eqnarray*}
592 |     \alpha(t) &=& (t,y_1); t \in [a,b]\\
593 |     \ell_1(t) &=& (b,t); t\in [y_1,y_2]\\
594 |     \beta(t) &=& (t,y_2); t \in [a,b]\\
595 |     \ell_2(t) &=& (a,t); t \in [y_1,y_2]\\
596 |   \end{eqnarray*}
597 |   and some calculus.
598 | \end{proof}

610 | \begin{defn}[Translation lengths]\label{defn:translation-lengths}
611 |   Let $\churro$ be attached to the waffles in
612 |   $\{\waffle_i\mid 1\leq i \leq m_\churro\}$ with strands
613 |   $\waffle_i \cap \churro =\strand_i$. We have
614 |   $\Gamma_\churro = \bk{h_\churro}$ which maps each $\strand_i$ to
615 |   itself and we define the \define{strand translation
616 |     lengths}:\[ \tau_{\strand_i} = d_{\waffle_i}(x,h_\churro x),
617 |     \textrm{~for any~} x \in \strand_i\] where $d_{\waffle_i}$ is the
618 |   standard unit edge length CAT(0) metric on $\waffle_i$. We further
619 |   define the \define{core translation length} to be the average\[
620 |     \tau_{\churrocore} = \frac{1}{m_\churro}\sum_{i=1}^{m_\churro}\tau_{\strand_i}
621 |   .\]
622 | \end{defn}
623 | 
624 | \begin{defn}[The churro metric]\label{defn:churro-metrization}
625 |   Let $\churro$ be as described above, then the flap $\flap_{i}$
626 |   connecting the core $\churrocore$ to the waffle $\waffle_i$ can be
627 |   metrized as:
628 |   \begin{enumerate}
629 |   \item A horostrip of width
630 |     \[\left|\ln\left(\frac{\tau_\churrocore}{\tau_{\strand_i}}\right)\right|\]
631 |     with the long side identified with $\churrocore$ if
632 |     $\tau_\churrocore > \tau_{\strand_i}$, or the long side identified
633 |     with $\strand_i \subset \waffle_i$ if
634 |     $\tau_\churrocore < \tau_{\strand_i}$; provided
635 |     $\tau_\churrocore \neq \tau_{\strand_i}$.
636 |   \item The Euclidean unit strip $\flap_{\bbE}=\{(x,y)\in \bbR^2 \mid
637 |     0\leq y\leq 1$ equipped with the Euclidean $\bbE^2$-metric, if
638 |     $\tau_\churrocore = \tau_{\strand_i}$.
639 |   \end{enumerate}
640 |   We then identify the sides of all the $\churrocore$ sides of the
641 |   flaps so that the lengths of any arc $\alpha$ contained in
642 |   $\churrocore$ coincide in ($\bbH^2$ or $\bbE^2$)-metric on each
643 |   attached flap.
644 | \end{defn}

646 | \begin{lem}\label{lem:ratio}
647 |   Let $\{\waffle_i\mid 1\leq i \leq m_\churro\}$ be the waffles
648 |   attached to a churro $\churro$, then the metric on $\churro$ given
649 |   in Definition \ref{defn:churro-metrization} is determined by the
650 |   collection of \define{clutching ratios of $\churro$}:
651 |   \[
652 |     r_{ij}=\frac{\tau_{\strand_i}}{\tau_{\strand_j}}; 1\leq i,j \leq m_\churro.
653 |   \]
654 | \end{lem}
655 | \begin{proof}
656 |   Once $r_{ij}$ is given, all the $\tau_{\strand_i}$ can be determined
657 |   as $\mu\alpha_i$ up to a common unknown scaling factor $\mu$, and
658 |   therefore the average $\tau_{\churrocore}$ also be determined as
659 |   $\mu\bar\alpha$ up to the same scaling factor $\mu$.  The metric on
660 |   $\churro$ is given by the ratios
661 |   $\frac{\tau_\churrocore}{\tau_{\strand_i}}=\frac{\bar\alpha}{\alpha_i}$,
662 |   which do not depend on $\mu$.
663 | \end{proof}

665 | \subsubsection{The path metric on a churro-waffle space}
666 | 
667 | Before continuing we note that churros, with the metric of Definition
668 | \ref{defn:churro-metrization}, may not have isometrically embedded
669 | boundaries in the following sense: the path metric restricted to the
670 | boundary (i.e. given by paths lying in the boundary) may not agree
671 | with the induced subspace metric; the reason being that horospheres
672 | are not convex.
673 | 
674 | \begin{defn}[Global path metric]\label{defn:path-metric.}
675 |   Let $\chuwaf{\Gamma}$ be the churro-waffle space for a clean group
676 |   $\Gamma$ where each waffle $\waffle$ is equipped with the standard
677 |   piecewise Euclidean path metric $ds_{\waffle}$ and each churro
678 |   $\churro$ is equipped with the path metric $ds_{\churro}$ given in
679 |   Definition \ref{defn:churro-metrization}. If a churro $\churro$ is
680 |   adjacent to a waffle $\waffle$ with $\churro \cap \waffle = \strand$
681 |   we identify the component $\gamma \subset \partial \churro$ with
682 |   $\strand \subset \waffle$ so that for any arc $\gamma\subset\strand$
683 |   we have \[
684 |     \int_\gamma ds_{\waffle} = \int_\gamma ds_{\churro}.
685 |     \] This induces the \define{global path metric $d$ on $\chuwaf\Gamma$.}
686 | \end{defn}
687 | 
688 | \begin{prop}\label{prop:global-isometry}
689 |   $\Gamma$ can be made to act by isometries on $\chuwaf\Gamma$ with
690 |   respect to the global path metric $d$.
691 | \end{prop}
692 | \begin{proof}
693 |   The only fact we actually need to show is that for a churro
694 |   $\churro$, the stabilizer $\Gamma_\churro = \bk{h_\churro}$ can act
695 |   by isometries. Indeed, we already know that for each waffle
696 |   $\waffle$ the path metric is $\Gamma_\waffle$-equivariant, if this
697 |   is also the case for every churro then, by construction and
698 |   definition, $\Gamma$ will carry geodesics to geodesics.
699 | 
700 |   Let $\{\waffle_i\mid 1\leq i \leq m_\churro\}$ be the set of waffles
701 |   attached to the churro $\churro$ and let $x \in \churrocore$ be some
702 |   point in the churro core. Let $x_i \in \waffle_i$ be the point in
703 |   connected to $x$ via a vertical leaf in the flap $\flap_{i}$
704 |   connecting $\churrocore$ to $\strand_i \subset \waffle_i$.
705 | 
706 |   Let $y_i$ be the point in $\churrocore$ connected to
707 |   $h_\churro \cdot x_i$ by a vertical leaf in the flap
708 |   $\flap_{i}$. We note that with respect to the path metric defined in
709 |   Definition \ref{defn:path-metric.} the length of the arc
710 |   $\alpha_i \subset \strand_i$ connecting $x_i$ to
711 |   $h_\churro\cdot x_i$ is exactly $\tau_{\strand_i}$ and by Definition
712 |   \ref{defn:churro-metrization} and Proposition
713 |   \ref{prop:quadrilaterals}, with respect to the path metric, in each
714 |   flap we have that the length of the arc $\beta$ between $x$ and
715 |   $y_i$ is $\tau_\churrocore$. It follows that restricted to each
716 |   flap $\flap_i$ the action of $h_\churro$ can be realized as the
717 |   restriction of a parabolic isometry of the upper half plane model of
718 |   $\bbH^2$ of the type:
719 |   \[ (x,y) \mapsto (x+h,y); \textrm{~for some~} h \in \bbR.
720 |   \] which agree on \[
721 |     \churrocore = \bigcap_{i=1}^{m_\churro} \flap_i.
722 |   \] It follows that the length of any parameterized curve can be in
723 |   $\chuwaf\Gamma$ can be computed using the local path metrics in the
724 |   churro and waffle pieces and that such lengths are preserved under
725 |   actions by $\Gamma$.
726 | \end{proof}

731 | \begin{defn}[Strand subdivision]\label{defn:waffle-subdivision}
732 |   Let $\waffle$ be a waffle, then a \define{strand subdivision} is a
733 |   combinatorial subdivision of the cube complex $\waffle$ so that each
734 |   strand lies inside the 1-skeleton of the subdivided waffle. The
735 |   \define{waffle subdivision} of a strand is the simplicial structure
736 |   on a strand induced by the strand subdivision of the ambient waffle.
737 | \end{defn}
738 | 
739 | \begin{defn}[Flap subdivision, core and strand vertices]
740 |   Let a waffle $\waffle$ be attached to a churro $\churro$ and if
741 |   $\hat\waffle$ is a strand subdivision of $\waffle$ and $\hat\strand$
742 |   is the corresponding waffle subdivision of the
743 |   strand $\strand = \waffle \cap \churro$, then we subdivide the flap
744 |   $\flap{}$ connecting $\strand$ and the churro core $\churrocore$ so
745 |   that each vertical leaf $\ell$ containing a vertex of $\hat\strand$
746 |   becomes a 1-cell called a \define{subdivision edge}. Extremities
747 |   of these 1-cells in $\churrocore$ are called \define{core vertices}
748 |   if they lie in the churro core, and \define{strand vertices} if they
749 |   lie in a strand.
750 | \end{defn}
751 | 
752 | \begin{defn}[Generalized Dehn twists]
753 |   Consider the map $\alpha:Z \to \churrocore$ identifying a component
754 |   of the boundary $Z \subset \partial \flap{}\subset \churro$ of a
755 |   flap of churro $\churro$ to the churro core $\churrocore$. A
756 |   \define{generalized Dehn twist} is the result of reattaching
757 |   $\flap{}$ to $\churrocore$ via a $\Gamma$-equivariant post
758 |   composition with a
759 |   translation:\[ (t\mapsto t+\epsilon)\circ \alpha; \epsilon \in
760 |     \mathbb R. \] An \define{equivariant generalized Dehn twist} of a
761 |   churro-waffle space $\chuwaf\Xi$ is the result of
762 |   $\aut{\chuwaf\Xi}$-equivariantly redefining the attaching maps of
763 |   flaps to churro cores via generalized Dehn twists. If $D$ is a
764 |   finite set of equivariant generalized Dehn twists, then denote the space
765 |   obtained by successively applying these as \[ \chuwaf\Xi^D.\]
766 | \end{defn}
767 | 
768 | \begin{lem}\label{lem:partial-twist}
769 |   There is a $\Gamma$-equivariant quasi-isometry between
770 |   $\chuwaf\Gamma$ and the result of finitely many equivariant
771 |   generalized Dehn twist $D$, furthermore $\Gamma$ still acts on the
772 |   resulting space $\chuwaf\Gamma^D$ by automorphisms. Also, given an
773 |   equivariant strand subdivision of each waffle in $\chuwaf\Gamma$,
774 |   equivariant generalized Dehn twists can only produce a finite number
775 |   of distinct (up to isomorphism) flap subdivisions that minimize the
776 |   number of $\Gamma$-orbits of core vertices.
777 | \end{lem}
778 | \begin{proof}
779 |   $\Gamma$ acts freely on $\chuwaf\Gamma$ and is in bijective
780 |   correspondence with homotopy classes of paths from some basepoint
781 |   $b \in \chuwaf\Gamma$ not lying in a churro core and some other
782 |   point $\gamma\cdot b \in \chuwaf\Gamma$ where $\gamma \in
783 |   \Gamma$. Let $\rho$ be a path connecting $b$ and $\gamma\cdot b$. We
784 |   can subdivide $\rho$ into
785 |   \begin{equation}\label{eqn:rho-subdiv}
786 |      \rho = \rho_1*\cdots*\rho_k
787 |   \end{equation}
788 |   where each $\rho_i$ is maximal with respect to containment with the
789 |   property that the interior of $\rho_i$ is disjoint from the set of
790 |   churro cores. Let $\delta$ be a generalized equivariant Dehn
791 |   twist. Each subpath of $\rho$ given in (\ref{eqn:rho-subdiv}) has an
792 |   image in the resulting space, the \define{push-forward of $\rho$} is
793 |   the concatenation
794 |   \[ \delta^*(\rho) = \rho_1*\mu_1*\cdots*\mu_{k-1}*\rho_k,
795 |   \] where the $\mu_i$ are shortest paths contained in churro cores connecting the
796 |   endpoints of the $\rho_{i}$ to $\rho_{i+1}$. It is a consequence of
797 |   the definition of fundamental groups and covering spaces that in
798 |   this way $\delta^*$ gives an action of $\Gamma$ by deck
799 |   transformations, and by construction the path metric remains
800 |   $\Gamma$-equivariant.
801 | 
802 |   Now note that the number of $\Gamma$ orbits of core vertices is
803 |   minimized whenever the endpoints of different subdivision edges in
804 |   different flaps coincide in $\churrocore$. Since $\Gamma$ acts
805 |   cocompactly on $\chuwaf\Gamma$, there are only finitely many
806 |   possible cellular decompositions of churros that can arise from
807 |   generalized equivariant Dehn twists, up to automorphism.
808 | \end{proof}

810 | \begin{lem}[Assembly Lemma]\label{lem:assemble}
811 |   Let $H,\Gamma$ be clean $\grsg$-groups and suppose there is a
812 |   quasi-isometry $\phi:\chuwaf H \to \chuwaf \Gamma$ that satisfies
813 |   the following:
814 |   \begin{enumerate}
815 |   \item\label{lem:assemble-tree} $\phi$ coarsely maps churros to churros and waffles to waffles, which induces an graph isomorphism of the underlying tree structures of $\chuwaf H$ and $\chuwaf \Gamma$.
816 | 
817 |   \item\label{lem:assemble-isom} The restriction of $\phi$ to a waffle $\waffle\subset\chuwaf H$ induces an isometry $\sqphi_{\waffle}$ of
818 |     $\waffle$ with respect to the intrinsic metric.
819 | 
820 |   \item\label{lem:assemble-clutch} $\phi$ preserves the clutching ratios of a churro, in that
821 |   \begin{equation}
822 |     \label{eq:4}
823 |     \frac{\tau_{\strand_1}}{\tau_{\strand_2}}=
824 |     \frac{\tau_{\strand_{1'}}}{\tau_{\strand_{2'}}}.
825 |   \end{equation}
826 |   for any pair $\waffle_1,\waffle_2\subset\chuwaf H$ of waffles attached to a churro $\churro$ along strands $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ respectively, where $\strand_{1'},\strand_{2'}$ are the respective strands along which $\sqphi_{\waffle_1}(\waffle_1),\sqphi_{\waffle_2}(\waffle_2)$ are attached to their shared adjacent churro (which is a bounded distance from $\phi(\churro)$).
827 | \end{enumerate}
828 | Then there are families of equivariant generalized Dehn twists $D,E$
829 | such that $\chuwaf H^D$ and $\chuwaf \Gamma^E$ are isomorphic. Thus
830 | both $H$ and $\Gamma$ can be made to act freely by automorphisms on the common churro-waffle space $\chuwaf H^D\cong\chuwaf \Gamma^E$
831 | \end{lem}
832 | \begin{proof}
833 |   If we metrize $\chuwaf H$ and $\chuwaf\Gamma$ as in Definition
834 |   \ref{defn:path-metric.} then by Lemma \ref{lem:ratio} and equation
835 |   (\ref{eq:4}), the local metrics defined on the churros make
836 |   $\phi$-correspondent churros isometric. Note that the strands in the
837 |   waffles only depend on the isometry classes of the waffles, so we
838 |   can pick strand subdivisions for every waffle that is
839 |   $\Isom{\waffle}$-equivariant, and pick such a subdivision for every
840 |   isometry class of waffles occurring in $\chuwaf H$ and $\chuwaf\Gamma$. It now follows by Lemma \ref{lem:partial-twist}, that
841 |   after performing finitely many equivariant generalized Dehn twists,
842 |   and redefining the flap subdivision, we can make $\chuwaf H$ and
843 |   $\chuwaf\Gamma$ isometric as metric spaces and isomorphic as cell
844 |   complexes.
845 | \end{proof}

953 |   $\Box$. Let $D_1$ be the largest cube diameter in
954 |   $\waffle_1$. Then there are strands $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$

961 |   $\coarseint{\sigma_1}{D_1}{\sigma_2}$ is bounded by some $D_2$.
