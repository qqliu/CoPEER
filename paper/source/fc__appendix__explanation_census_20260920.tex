\section{From a counterexample to a complete explanation: thirteen follow-up packages}
\label{app:explanation-census}

This study followed every dossier in the earlier 285-dossier collection whose annotations described both a failure family and a repair: thirteen in all. The square-root result had already been formalized; for the other twelve, human contributors and coding agents developed formal proofs, followed by separate adversarial model reviews. All thirteen packages contain Lean-checked family and repair statements with explicit assumptions.

Table~\ref{tab:explanation-census} lists all thirteen. Ten of the source dossiers are in the 147-dossier human-approved core; the square-root, array-assignment, and recurrence-sum dossiers are outside it. The packages are mathematical developments of the selected examples, each with its own review record and scope (Appendix~\ref{app:recent-human-review}).

\paragraph{Three outcomes.}
The packages illustrate three outcomes, which can overlap. One is a complete classification of failures: the square-root package proves that the original algorithm fails exactly at the positive perfect squares and, separately, that the corrected algorithm is correct on every allowed input. Another is a corrected local statement: in the signed-cut example, at the source-compatible values $n=3$, $T=2$, $d=20$, $f=-1$, and $a=19$, the source's total $T+f$ is $1$ while the correct additive total $T+a-2(n-2)d$ is $-19$. Because the surrounding argument needs a positive total, the package also proves an exact positivity condition (Lean statements \path{repair} and \path{exact_positivity}). The third is a proof of the intended final statement after the faulty step is replaced, which the cycle-deletion, prime-residue, and recursive-increment packages supply. Proving a family or repair statement is not the same as proving the source's conclusion; the middle column of the table says which was done.

\paragraph{Assumptions matter.}
The adjacency-power family includes diagonal matrix entries, while applying it to the source's off-diagonal assertion needs the narrower family with $n\ge3$; in the liveness example, the equivalence fixes a prefix that includes the violated request, and letting the prefix end earlier changes the statement. The saved model reviews record these distinctions next to the formal proofs.
