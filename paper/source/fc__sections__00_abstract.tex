\begin{abstract}
An objection to a mathematical proof requires evidence of its own: a critic can misread a claim, overlook an assumption, or reject a valid step. Counterexamples provide checkable evidence by refuting a false assertion even inside a proof of a true theorem. We introduce Co-PEER, a workflow for constructing and checking \emph{proofs of error}. Each \emph{objection dossier} records the challenged source claim, its assumptions, and a concrete counterexample. It includes a Lean-checked proof that the encoded assumptions hold and the encoded conclusion fails, and a review of whether the formal statement represents the source claim. BugBank stores these dossiers and their revision histories. A human-approved core of 147 dossiers drawn from 137 distinct sources supports a benchmark of 100 proof texts known to contain an error. The strongest evaluated LLM critic selects a reference line (a line annotated as incorrect) as its first choice in 73.0\% of texts, compared with 18.0\% for the best of four simple text rules. We also test whether a checked counterexample helps models complete Lean repair tasks in which the correction to prove is given. Finally, with the help of AI tools, we develop selected counterexamples into explanations of entire families of failures. We characterize and count the frozen six-colorings of doubled odd cycles of length at least five. We prove that a two-threshold coloring of the integers is the unique optimum within its family and exceeds a conjectured bound. We also determine every input on which a printed integer square-root algorithm fails and prove the correction proposed in the source.
\end{abstract}
