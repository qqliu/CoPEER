The proof claims it's impossible. What if we take g=2? Then 4(g-1)=4, 6(g-1)=6. So we need a harmonic group action on a genus 2 graph with order >4, but not 6. Possible orders: 5? But group orders for genus 2 harmonic actions? The paper's Example in section~Hurwitz: "The following example shows that the upper bound 6(g-1) is attained for g=2: let T be the rooted tree... The group ℤ/3ℤ × ℤ/2ℤ = ⟨σ,τ⟩ acts harmonically on G, achieving the Hurwitz genus bound." So order 6 is achieved. Are there harmonic actions of order 5 on genus 2 graphs? If such exists, and |Γ|=5 > 4(2-1)=4, then Proposition 6.1 would be false because |Γ|=5 ≠ 6. But does the paper's assumptions allow order 5? Let's check the prime factors lemma: "If p is a prime factor of |Γ|, then p divides (g+1)!." For g=2, (g+1)! = 6, so prime factors can only be 2 or 3. So order 5 is impossible for g=2 by that lemma.
