   Are there any cross-equivalence? e1 not~ e4, etc. So we have exactly two ~-classes: C1 = {e1,e2,e3}, C2 = {e4,e5,e6}.

   Now, "each vertex of G is in at most two ~-classes." What does "vertex of G is in a ~-class" mean? Let's interpret: a vertex v ∈ V(G) is "in" a ~-class C if v is an endpoint of some edge in C. Or maybe v is one of the vertices whose edges form the class? Let's check both interpretations.

   Interpretation A: v is in C if v is an endpoint of at least one edge in C.
   - Vertex 1: incident to edges e1=12, e2=13. Both are in C1. So 1 is in C1. Not in C2 (since C2 edges are 34,35,45, none incident to 1). So 1 is in 1 class.
   - Vertex 2: incident to e1=12, e3=23. Both in C1. So 2 in 1 class.
   - Vertex 3: incident to e2=13, e3=23, e4=34, e5=35. Edges in C1: e2,e3. Edges in C2: e4,e5. So 3 is in both C1 and C2! That's 2 classes.
