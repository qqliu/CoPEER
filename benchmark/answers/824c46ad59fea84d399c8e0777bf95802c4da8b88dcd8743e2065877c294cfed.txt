So, in a connected graph, for any two vertices, there is a walk of length $n-1$ between them. Therefore, $(I_{n} + A)^{n-1}$ has no 0s.

Now, let's reconsider the second part. The claim is that if $(I_{n} + A)^{n-1}$ has no 0s, then the graph is connected.

Suppose the graph is disconnected. Then there are at least two components, and there are no walks between vertices in different components. For any two vertices in different components, the $(i,j)$ entry of $(I_{n} + A)^{n-1}$ would be 0, since there are no walks between them of any length.

Therefore, if $(I_{n} + A)^{n-1}$ has no 0s, it must be that the graph is connected.

So, both parts of the proof are correct, and the main claim is true.
